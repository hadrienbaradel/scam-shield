# Breach exposure
Status: Partial · Raised by Vlad, 2026-10-27 · Discussed: yes (2026-11-04) · Last updated: 2026-11-04

## Why
Scammers quote real data (an IBAN, a social-security number, an address) to look genuine. If the senior knows which breaches leaked their data, that "proof" loses its power. The life context lets the senior tick eight well-known breaches; since Nov 4 the senior can also ask Prudence to look their own email address up in a public breach list, and the verdict uses what came back ("that bank number is real, but it leaked in the Bouygues Telecom breach").

## Behavior
1. **Settings row.** Under "Data breaches you were part of", the senior sees whether Prudence has looked their address up ("Not looked up yet", or "Looked up 2 minutes ago: your address is in 3 known breaches") and one button, "Look up my email address" ("Look up again" after a first time), with one sentence that says what happens: Prudence sends their email address, and nothing else, to a public list of known breaches, and nothing is sent until they tap. The protection score on Today counts the lookup as one of its criteria (step "Look up my email address", `protection-menu.md` line 2b), and a lookup that found no breach counts too: the answer is what protects.
2. **Nothing is sent before the tap.** The lookup never runs on its own: not at sign-in, not when the mailbox is read. Once the senior has asked for it, "Look through my mailbox again" refreshes it too.
3. **What was found is listed and removable.** Each breach found is added to the list with the company and the year ("Bouygues Telecom (2025)"), ticked, next to the eight well-known ones; unticking removes it from the verdicts like them.
4. **What leaked is kept in plain words** (bank account number, address, phone, date of birth, social security number, old password...), so that a message quoting real data gets the reason "That bank number is real, but it leaked in the Bouygues Telecom breach (2025). Thieves use it to look credible." The same for an old password, a social security number (US, or French when announced as such), a date of birth after "born on" or "née le", and the last digits of a card; the reason outranks the link on the card, so the senior reads it (the verdict engine's demo path). Digits alone, not announced, are not a number.
5. **Which list.** Without a Have I Been Pwned key, Prudence asks XposedOrNot (free, no key) which breaches hold the address, and completes what leaked and the company's name from Have I Been Pwned's free catalogue (which knows, for instance, that the Bouygues 2025 breach included bank account numbers); with a key, it asks Have I Been Pwned's own search. The senior never reads a provider's name: the sentence says "a public list of known breaches".
6. **Plain answers, always.** An address in no breach says "No known breach has your email address" and changes nothing; a list that is busy or unreachable says "The breach list did not answer. Try again in a minute", never a technical error; a second tap within a minute says to wait.
7. **Welcome step.** The breach step of the welcome offers the same button under the checkboxes; skipping it is one tap on Next (nothing sent).
8. **What each breach means, where the list is.** Each breach in Settings and in the welcome says what leaked in the senior's words ("Leaked: bank account number, address") and the list ends with one sentence: "A message that quotes one of these is not proof it is genuine: thieves buy leaked details by the thousand." The Learn tab has the lesson "They know your real details" (`leaked-details`, ninth; its French twin opens the Bouygues debit notice). Viamedis / Almerys 2024 (33 million French people: name, date of birth, social security number, health insurer) joins the curated list. Breach news (a new breach in the papers, with the one thing to do about it) reaches the public watch page through the scam watch (`public-database.md`, lines 8 to 10), not the senior's app. Not built: a screen of its own, impots.gouv without a confirmed source.
9. **Nothing about the senior's breaches reaches the trusted person** unless the senior asks about a message: no guardian screen or email reads the life context; the breach shows up only inside the reasons of a verdict the senior chose to share.

## Demo path
Settings → "Data breaches you were part of" → "Look up my email address" (2 s): "Looked up just now: your address is in 1 known breach", "Bouygues Telecom (2025)" ticked. Then the €69.90 debit notice: "That bank number is real, but it leaked in the Bouygues Telecom breach (2025)." Ten seconds; the breach is the story. The demo mailbox's address must be in a real breach for the live lookup; otherwise tick Bouygues by hand, the card is the same.

## Out of scope
Monitoring the dark web; infostealer logs (Hudson Rock's free lookup returns masked passwords and machine names, nothing a senior should see); identity-theft insurance; changing passwords for the user; scanning the senior's other accounts; a lookup by phone number.

## Open questions
- XposedOrNot's free tier is "for personal and low-volume use" (2 a second, 25 an hour, 100 a day per server address); a product at scale needs its paid plan or a Have I Been Pwned key ($4.39 a month): who pays, and from which user count?
- Should "what leaked" be shown to the trusted person to help them judge a message?
- Source of breach news without a per-person list (impots.gouv, Ameli, health data): CNIL, cybermalveillance, a curated list?
