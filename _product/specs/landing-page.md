# Landing page: heyprudence.com for people who do not know Prudence yet
Status: Partial (the early-access request and its console page are built; the page waits for Hadrien's file) · Raised by Vlad, 2026-11-04 · Decided by Vlad 2026-11-04 (built from the pitch material and the app's design system the same day, at Vlad's call at 13:00 ET, Hadrien's `prudence-site` page not being in the repo; his page folds in as the How it works section when it lands; the adult child is the reader; the demo and an early-access request are the two doors, since Google sign-in is still in its test phase) · Last updated: 2026-11-04

## Why
heyprudence.com is live since Nov 4 and is the link judges, journalists and families open. Signed out, the root shows the app's one-screen welcome, which assumes the visitor already knows Prudence. A visitor should understand in a minute what Prudence does for a parent, see it judge messages that look real, and leave with one of two things done: the demo tried, or early access asked for. The public database is the other reason to come and to link to us: a lookup, the library, the week's breaches.

## Behavior
1. Signed out, `https://heyprudence.com/` shows the landing page as plain HTML: readable without scripts, one page and one stylesheet, the font served from our host, no tracker and no script from another origin. A signed-in senior, trusted person or admin never sees it: they land on Today, People I protect or the console as before, without a flash of the landing; a link with a hash (`#/demo`, an invitation) goes straight to the app.
2. The page has a How it works section: until Hadrien's `prudence-site` page (what it does, how it works with the app's screenshots from demo data, the Meta models, the measured results with their limits, safety, team, provenance) reaches the repo, that section is three short steps with the app's own screenshots (`_product/design/2026-11-04-redesign/`); his page replaces it when it lands and keeps its no-script rule. The page is written to the adult child who sets Prudence up, in the second person, at the app's type size (19 px or larger) and in plain words, so the parent can read it too; English and French, the language button as in the app.
3. Above the fold: the question "Is this message real, for you?", one sentence on what Prudence does (checks every new email against your parent's own life: her orders, her bank, her providers, the people she writes to; says it in plain words; moves a trap out of the way, and anything can be put back), and two buttons: "Try it with Linda's inbox" (the demo, `#/demo`) and "Protect my parent's email" (the early-access form, line 6).
4. Examples: three messages from Linda's fictional day (the Bank of America lock notice, Ethan's "send $300 today, don't tell Mom", the USPS fee), each shown as the message and the verdict card Prudence gives it (Scam, the reason in plain words, what to do instead), in the look of the app's card; every sender and link is fictional (reserved `.test` names) and printed as text, never clickable.
5. The differentiator and the promises: "A message is only a scam relative to a life. Prudence knows hers.", with three lines of life context (your parcel comes by Amazon Logistics, not Chronopost; this is Ethan's name, not Ethan's number; that account number leaked in a breach, so showing it proves nothing); the three promises (scams are labeled and moved to a Prudence folder, nothing is ever deleted; any message comes back with one tap; the trusted person sees only what she chose to share); "Ask my trusted person" in one line with the answer-by-one-tap.
6. Early access: "Protect my parent's email" opens a short form on the page (the Gmail address to protect, whose it is: my parent's or mine, a first name optional, a note optional) under the line "Prudence is in Google's test phase: we add you by hand, usually within a day, then you sign in with Google from this page." Sending stores the request (`POST /api/early-access`, 10 a minute from one place) and shows "Thank you. We will write to <address>."; a malformed address gets a plain line; a second request for the same address gets the same thank-you and stores nothing more. The console lists the requests (`/admin/#/early-access`: date, address, whose, name, note, language, status open or added, one button "Mark added"); the server mails nobody; a request is deleted 90 days after it was marked added (`src/jobs/retention.js`); the privacy page says what is kept and for how long. The settings, onboarding and score rule does not apply: this is a visitor's request, not a capability over a senior's life, so the app shows nothing for it.
7. Proof for the child, one block with its sources: on 18,849 held-back public emails, phishing called Scam 81% with Muse Spark against 29% with rules alone, 1 genuine email in 17,291 called Scam, the median verdict time; Meta's Muse Spark named and visible (Hadrien's Meta models table). Numbers live in this block only; everywhere else the page uses words, as the app's no-percentages rule asks.
8. Three doors into the public database in their own section, "What scammers are doing this week", introduced as the other half of a verdict (what is known of the world): a lookup box "Check a website, a sender address or a phone number" that opens `/public/#/suspects?q=<text>`; "Browse the scam library: <N> examples in 21 families, free to reuse (CC BY 4.0)" to `/public/#/library`, N read live from the library; "This week's breaches and new techniques" to `/public/#/watch`.
9. Footer: privacy, terms, the public repository, "Built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon 2026, Indie track", the operator and the contact point, the language switch; no cookie banner, because the visitor's browser keeps nothing beyond the language choice.
10. Phone first: no horizontal scroll at 360 px, type 19 px or larger, tap targets 64 px, contrast AA (AAA on the verdict words), landmarks, labels and focus order for a screen reader, and the page shows its first screen in under a second on a phone connection (screenshots sized for their slot, lazy below the fold).

## Demo path
Not in the Nov 4 video (rendered). For judges and the X post: open heyprudence.com, read the question, tap "Try it with Linda's inbox", come back, type a sender address into the lookup box, open the library.

## Out of scope
A blog, pricing, a newsletter, analytics or any tracker, an app subdomain (the app keeps `/`), Google verification itself, mailing the early-access requester (Resend later), A/B tests, a sign-in button for strangers while the client is in its test phase.

## Acceptance
- `tests/landing.test.js`: `/` without a session answers 200 with the landing landmarks (the question, both buttons, three example cards, the lookup form to `/public/#/suspects`, the library and watch links, the footer links) and no script from another origin.
- `tests/early-access.test.js` (built Nov 4): a request is stored once, a duplicate gets the same answer and stores nothing more, a malformed address or one carrying `?`, `%`, `&`, `=` or `/` is 400, the rate limit stores nothing, the console list and Mark added are admins only, retention deletes a request 90 days after it was marked added and keeps open ones.
- Headless Chrome at 1280 and 360 px in both languages: no horizontal overflow, zero console errors (the harness outside the repo); noted in the Done line.
- Reading check: one person outside the team says what the page asks them to do.
