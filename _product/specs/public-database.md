# Public database: scam library, suspect list, scam watch
Status: Partial · Raised by Vlad, 2026-11-04 · Decided by Vlad 2026-11-04 (built with subagents the same day; plan: `docs/plans/2026-11-04-public-database.md`) · Last updated: 2026-11-04

## Why
Prudence judges a message against the senior's life; the other half of a good verdict is what scammers are doing this week. A public, openly licensed database of scam examples, the sites and numbers they use, and the breaches and new techniques behind them gives Prudence that "world context", gives the model its evaluation and few-shot material, gives the Learn tab its stories, and gives journalists, banks and other apps a reason to link to us. Nobody publishes one today (`_product/research/public-database.md`). Desktop, at `/public/` on heyprudence.com, for people who work on scams: another interface of the product with no sign-in, never the senior's app.

## Behavior
Library
1. A visitor browses scam examples at `/public/#/library` by family (21 families: the 20 plus recovery, `data/library/families.json`, each with its name and one-sentence definition in English and French and its references to the Fed ScamClassifier, IC3, FTC, Scamwatch, cybermalveillance and NFIB codes; a rail with counts), by channel (email, sms, chat, voice, web, letter), country, language and words (`GET /api/public/library?family&channel&country&language&q&page`, 50 a page, newest first); each entry page (`GET /api/public/library/:id`) shows the redacted message in a monospace block with its links printed as text (never clickable), the family and its definition, the brand impersonated, the lure, the ask, where and when it was first and last seen, its source and licence with attribution, Prudence's verdict with its reasons (or the line that a corpus entry was classified, not judged), the active pattern cards that impersonate the same brand, and a Look up button for each sender, site and number that opens the suspect lookup.
2. Anyone submits an example at `/public/#/submit` (`POST /api/public/library/submit`, 10 a minute per address): the form says "Remove your own name and details before sending; the scam's links, numbers and sender stay: they are the evidence. What you send is redacted again, classified and read by a reviewer before it appears. Published under CC BY 4.0."; the message is redacted before it is stored (`redactExample`: bank account, card and social security numbers, one-time codes, a quoted password, email addresses in the text, in link text and in a link's path, a link's query string, credentials and tokens; the sender, the links' host and path and phone numbers stay as evidence), hashed on its words to refuse a duplicate (409 with the entry's id, or with the submitter's own pending id, never another submitter's), classified by Muse Spark into a family with the brand, lure, ask, language and country (`lib/libraryMuse.js`; a model outage never blocks a submission), and published only after a reviewer approves it on the admin console's Library page (the one place the console shows a message: publishing it is the point); the submitter reads the outcome at `#/submit?status=<id>` (pending, approved with a link to the entry, rejected with the reason, withdrawn). A rejected or withdrawn submission keeps nothing of the message but its decision and its word hash; the submitter's address is kept only as a keyed hash (for the duplicate answer) until the decision; decided submissions are deleted after 30 days, unreviewed ones after 180 (`src/jobs/retention.js`). Links in an entry are http, https or mailto only, with their query string, credentials, tokens (a UUID, a JWT, a long run with digits) and addresses redacted. No rule proves a first name absent: `validateEntry` warns ("may carry a name") and the Library page shows the words to read twice before Approve; the human review before publishing stays the safeguard. The GitHub-issue path is not built.
3. The library ships with a seed: the 32 demo cases that are not Safe (`scripts/library-seed.mjs` writes `data/library/entries/lib-demo-<id>.json`, redacted with the persona's name, lure, ask and verdict lines in English, the message in its own language) and the redacted, classified samples of the CC BY corpora that `scripts/library-import.mjs` brings in (UCI SMS Spam Collection, Mishra-Soni smishing; agent D's phase, the counts in `docs/PROVENANCE.md`); the 7 pattern cards are served by `GET /api/public/cards` and joined to entries by brand, not copied; the raw corpora never enter the repo.
4. Every entry is CC BY 4.0 JSON in the public repo (one file per entry with its source, licence and attribution, every file validated when loaded: a bad one fails `make test`); the whole library downloads as one file at `GET /api/public/library.json` (`licence`, `generated`, families, sources, entries), linked from the footer, the results line, every entry page and About; About names the operator, the contact point (`PUBLIC_DB_OPERATOR`, `PUBLIC_DB_CONTACT`), the licence and the sources.
5. From the senior's side: `POST /api/library/share` (signed in, 20 a minute) sends the redacted message once with her explicit tap, with her verdict, her name replaced by `[name]` and her own address as the sender by `[someone]@domain`; `GET /api/library/shared` gives `{ count, pending, approved }` for Settings ("Shared with the library: 3 messages") and `POST /api/library/shared/withdraw` sets her pending rows to withdrawn ("Stop sharing"); the welcome asks nothing, consent is per message. Built: the API; the tap under a verdict and the Settings row are a To do for the redesign session (`_product/log.md`).
Suspect list
6. A visitor looks up a website, a sender address or a phone number (`/public/#/suspects?q=`, `GET /api/public/suspects?q=`: a link pasted whole, an address in any case, a number with spaces, dots or dashes; numbers compare on their last 9 digits) and sees "Reported N times, last seen <date>, weight W" with the library entries that used it, each opening its page; a report counts 1 within 90 days of its last sighting, 0.5 within a year, 0.25 after; a listing never says "scam" without an entry behind it (the empty state says so), and a private person's number is never listed on its own: indicators come only from published entries, never from a Be careful entry, a brand's own address, a reserved or private host or a `[someone]@` placeholder.
7. Anyone can contest a listing from the same page (`POST /api/public/suspects/contest`, five a minute from one place: the four elements of a notice, name, email, the exact address of the listing (filled in), the reason, plus the good-faith statement; a missing element is named); the listing shows "contested" at once and a decision with its reason follows within 7 days from the console's Contests page (upheld removes the listing and the entries stay, dismissed keeps it), read back by the notice's id (`GET /api/public/suspects/contest/:id`, `#/suspects?contest=<id>`; nothing is mailed); the operator and the contact point are named on the site and under the form.
Scam watch
8. Once a day the Scam watch job (`src/jobs/scamwatch.js`, listed in Jobs next to the threat feed) reads fifteen sources: Have I Been Pwned's breach catalogue (the breaches added in the last 30 days) and its latest-breaches feed, ransomware.live, IC3 industry alerts, CISA news, NCSC UK, Europol, cybermalveillance's fiches réflexes, Krebs on Security, BleepingComputer, The Record, Malwarebytes Labs, Google Threat Intelligence, Which? (its scam stories only) and Mastodon's public #scam timeline. A HIBP breach is a breach by construction (who, how many, which data, verified or claimed, dates); every other item is classified by Muse Spark (`lib/watchMuse.js`) as a breach, a technique (channel, family, brand, lure, ask, first seen, new or variant, country, language) or something else, or waits unclassified for the reviewer when no model is configured. A dead source is one error line and the run goes on, and a feed body over 2 MB is that source's error line, so one bloated feed cannot hold the run; it fails only when every source failed. X, LinkedIn and Reddit are not read; Bluesky search (`BLUESKY_IDENTIFIER`, `BLUESKY_APP_PASSWORD`, declared, read by nothing yet) and the California AG list are follow-ups.
9. An item becomes public only when a reviewer approves it on `/admin/#/watch` (candidates first, the important ones on top, the classification and the importance reasons, what the feed said, the one action editable in English and French, Reject with a reason, "Fetch the feeds now" as a manual run of the job, the decided items and the sources with the last run; an item the classifier failed on is still shown so a person can decide). A decision is final: a second approve or reject on a decided item is refused (409); only a candidate, or an item the classifier failed on, takes one. "Important" is the explicit rule of `lib/watch.js`, never a model's feeling: a verified breach of 1M+ people or of passwords, bank, card, social-security, identity, health, date-of-birth, address or phone data, added within 30 days; or a technique not seen before, in the senior's country (US, FR) or everywhere. An approved technique becomes a candidate pattern card (`watch-<slug>`) through the threat feed's `cardFromAlert`, unless the threat feed carded that link already, so it can at most push a verdict to Be careful until a human activates it; feeding Breach exposure is not built.
10. `/public/#/watch` (`GET /api/public/watch?days=7`, up to 90) shows the approved items of the last 7, 30 or 90 days in two sections, Breaches and New techniques, each with its kind, Important and verified-or-claimed chips, the title linking to the source, the source and dates, the facts in plain words (people affected, data leaked, status; channel, family linking to the library, brand, lure, ask) and the one thing a senior should do in English with the French line under it: a headline, a link and our own words, never a copy of the article.

## Demo path
None for the Nov 4 video. Later: the library page with a family open, one entry and its verdict; the watch page with this week's breach and the line "that number is real, but it leaked in the X breach".

## Out of scope
Crowd-sourced bare numbers without evidence; a consumer app; the senior's own email checked against HIBP (Breach exposure); dark-web monitoring; scraping any site that forbids it; a separate database server (the repo is the database; approved submissions wait in Postgres, the export of approved rows to files is not built yet).

## Acceptance
- `tests/library.test.js`, `tests/library-api.test.js`: every seed file validates; a submission with an IBAN or a code is stored redacted, a duplicate answers 409, a stranger never gets another submitter's id, approve builds a valid entry once, admin endpoints are admins only, the share and withdraw endpoints count per senior.
- `tests/suspects.test.js`: three spellings of a phone number are one indicator, the age weights, contested and removed states, a contest missing one element is 400.
- `tests/watch.test.js`: a feed item never becomes public on its own; a dead source is one error line and every source dead fails the run; HIBP rows need no model; an approved technique makes a candidate card, never an active one.
- The pages: headless Chrome passes at 1280 and 360 px in both themes with zero console errors and every server value escaped (agents S and C, Nov 4, harness outside the repo); a live pass on heyprudence.com/public/ after the deploy is still to do.
