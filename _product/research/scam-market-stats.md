# Scam market statistics for the pitch (accessed 2026-10-27)

FTC (Consumer Sentinel) and FBI (IC3) count different things and overlap; never add them. Reported losses are a fraction of real losses (FTC's own estimates below). Figures from PDFs were extracted with pdftotext from the linked files.

## FTC Consumer Sentinel, 2024 (press release March 10, 2025; Data Book 2024)
- More than $12.5 billion reported lost to fraud in 2024, up 25% on 2023; about 2.6 million fraud reports; 38% of reporters lost money (27% in 2023). The Dec 2025 older-adults report restates the 2024 total as "about $12.8 billion" (later data).
- By category: investment scams $5.7B; imposter scams $2.95B (government imposters $789M); business and job opportunities $750.6M (job scams $501M).
- Contact method: email was the most reported contact method for the second year, phone calls second, text messages third (no loss split in the release; the Data Book table could not be extracted from the PDF).
- Text scams 2024: $470M reported lost to scams that started with a text, more than five times 2020; top five: fake package delivery, phony job offers, fake fraud alerts, unpaid-toll notices, "wrong number" texts (FTC Data Spotlight, April 14, 2025). Footnote: texts are opened up to 98% of the time versus 20% for email (FTC citing a 2022 FCC advisory report).
- Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024 ; https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024 ; https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/csn-annual-data-book-2024.pdf

## FTC, age 60+ in 2024 (Protecting Older Consumers 2024-2025, Dec 2025)
- 421,031 fraud reports with age data came from people 60+ (36% of those with age); 109,580 reported a loss; losses "nearly $2.4 billion" (about $1.9B in 2023, about $600M in 2020, +300%).
- Controlling for population, older adults were 5 times (400%) more likely than younger people to report losing money to a tech support scam, nearly 3 times (172%) on prize, sweepstakes or lottery scams, 39% more on romance scams, 36% more on government impersonation. Tech support losses by 60+: $159M.
- Median loss for 80+ exceeded $1,600. Aggregate 60+ losses were highest for scams starting on social media (nearly ninefold since 2020); median loss was highest for phone ($2,210 vs $650 social media); text-scam losses by older adults were up more than eightfold since 2020; people 80+ lost most to phone calls. 28% of 60+ text-scam loss reports were texts impersonating well-known businesses, mostly Amazon and banks.
- Estimated true 2024 cost to older adults: $10.1B to $81.5B depending on method.
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf

## FTC Consumer Sentinel, 2025 (testimony March 25, 2026; the 2025 Data Book was not found online on 2026-10-27)
- $15.9 billion reported lost in 2025 ("about $16 billion" in the Oct 2026 report), about 3 million fraud reports; imposter scams most reported (over 1 million reports, over $3.5B); investment scams the largest losses ($7.9B); losses up about 430% since 2020 (MLex summary).
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud ; https://www.mlexwatch.com/ftcwatch/articles/2463206
- Surfshark (not an official source, unverified): 2025 text-scam losses about $639M, +36%. https://surfshark.com/research/study/americans-lose-billions-to-online-scams

## FTC, age 60+ in 2025 (Protecting Older Consumers 2025-2026, Oct 19, 2026)
- 469,597 reports with age data from people 60+ (33%); about 123,000 with a loss; losses "over $3 billion" (from about $2.4B in 2024; +400% since 2020). Losses over $100,000 were 6% of 60+ loss reports but 70% of the dollars.
- Multipliers vs younger adults: tech support over 4 times (324%); prize, sweepstakes, lottery over 2 times (112%); romance +48%; government impersonation +15%; impersonating a friend or family member +15%.
- Investment losses by 60+ over $1B (+42%); median 60+ loss about $900; 80+ $2,000; phone median $3,000 vs $599 social media; 60+ text-scam aggregate losses +61% vs 2024; 32% of 60+ no-loss reports were text scams (DMV and toll impersonation) and 29% phone calls (Medicare, banks, Amazon, the FTC).
- Estimated true 2025 cost to older adults: $13.3B to $119.5B.
- Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/10/ftc-issues-annual-report-congress-agencys-actions-protect-older-adults ; https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2026-older-adults-report-10192026.pdf

## FBI IC3, 2024 (report April 2025)
- 859,532 complaints; $16.6B reported losses (+33% on 2023); 256,256 complaints with an actual loss.
- 60+: 147,127 complaints; $4.8B losses (secondary sources: $4.885B, +43%). 60+ losses by type: investment $1.834B; tech support $982.4M; confidence/romance $389.3M; BEC $385.0M; personal data breach $254.2M; government impersonation $208.1M; lottery/sweepstakes $75.9M; phishing/spoofing $20.2M. 60+ complaints by type: phishing/spoofing 23,252; tech support 16,777; extortion 12,618; personal data breach 9,827; investment 9,448; romance 7,626; government impersonation 4,521. Cryptocurrency descriptor: $2.839B. Emergency (grandparent) scams: $2.7M (ABC7 citing the FBI, secondary).
- Source: https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2024_IC3Report.pdf ; https://abc7news.com/post/seniors-lost-48-billion-scammers-2024-fbi/16231423/

## FBI IC3, 2025 (report April 2026)
- 1,008,597 complaints; $20.877B reported losses (+26%). Phishing/spoofing was the most reported crime type (191,561 complaints, $215.8M); tech/customer support 47,794 complaints, $2.135B; investment $8.649B. AI-related descriptor: 22,364 complaints, $893.3M.
- 60+: 201,266 complaints (+37%); $7.748B losses (+59%); average loss $38,500; 12,444 people lost more than $100,000. 60+ losses by type: investment $3.519B; tech/customer support $1.041B; confidence/romance $584.0M; BEC $568.0M; government impersonation $413.2M; personal data breach $324.5M; lottery/sweepstakes $136.3M; phishing/spoofing $77.0M. 60+ complaints by type: phishing/spoofing 48,064; tech support 21,333; investment 16,926; personal data breach 11,705; romance 10,188; extortion 9,111; government impersonation 8,628. Cryptocurrency: $4.347B; AI-related: 3,143 complaints, $352.5M. "Distress" (grandparent) scams: over $5M (HousingWire citing IC3, secondary).
- Source: https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf ; https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/ ; summary https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses.html

## Main scam types hitting seniors (both agencies combined)
- Investment (largest dollars, often starting on social media); tech support (highest likelihood multiplier, second in dollars; Microsoft and Norton impersonation); romance/confidence; government impersonation (Medicare, Social Security, the FTC itself); business impersonation (Amazon, banks; the top loss type by phone and by text); prize, sweepstakes and lottery; friend or family impersonation (grandparent scams, small in dollars, now +15% likelihood for 60+); phishing/spoofing (the most frequent complaint, the entry point to the others).

## Email and SMS as scam channels
- Email was the most reported contact method in 2024 (FTC); phishing/spoofing was the most reported crime type in 2025 overall and for 60+ (IC3).
- Text-initiated losses: $470M in 2024 (FTC); 60+ text-scam losses +61% in 2025; a third of 60+ no-loss reports in 2025 were about texts.
- Google: Phone and Messages flag more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts a month (Google blog, Oct 14, 2026, https://blog.google/security/new-android-scam-protections/). Truecaller: 12.9B messages screened and 20.3M tagged as scams in one week of Sept 2026 (TechCrunch). Gen: social engineering was over 80% of threats observed in the first three quarters of 2025 (Norton PR, Nov 3, 2025).
