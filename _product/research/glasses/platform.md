# Glasses platform: what Meta's glasses and the phone allow for a listening coach
Vlad's ask of 2026-10-29: glasses that listen to a doorstep pitch, a call on speaker or a negotiation, can see the phone, and whisper advice; do the APIs allow it, and can we get accepted in time. Written 2026-10-29 from the DAT 1.0 docs, both SDK repos and their issue trackers, Android and legal sources. It extends `research/detection-channels/meta-glasses-assistants.md` (2026-10-28), whose "look at the letter" plan, prices and setup steps stand. "#n" is an issue in the Android or iOS SDK repo: a developer report, with Meta's SDK team answering where quoted.

**Verdict.** Build "Listen" with zero glasses code: the phone's own microphone hears both people, Muse Voice Transcribe and Muse Spark judge, and Android `TextToSpeech` whispers through the glasses used as ordinary Bluetooth (A2DP) headphones. No approval, no Developer Mode, any earbuds work, so the Nov 1 cut cannot kill it. The glasses microphones are the wrong ear for the other person: HFP is beamformed to the wearer; the unbeamformed in-stream audio needs the camera on (LED lit), is beta-only, silences the glasses' speakers while it runs, and glasses release 129 ends any DAT session when HFP opens. Meta's SDK team, Oct 13: "routing voice input to the phone mic is the right call" (#192). "Look" (DAT camera) and voice launch stay optional Developer Mode layers. Calls stay on the second device of `live-calls.md`: the phone in a call reads silence.

## 1. DAT status on 2026-10-29
- 1.0.1 (2026-10-09) is still the latest on both CHANGELOGs (Android: HEVC buffer corruption and stream stutter fixed; iOS: "No developer-facing changes"). The Android repo moved to github.com/meta/meta-wearables-dat-android (the facebook/ URL redirects); its README still says "developer preview" while the FAQ says the preview ended with 1.0 (rollout from Sep 30). The documented pair is Meta AI V292 and firmware V128 on every model; the field now runs Meta AI 295 (Android), 296 (iOS) and glasses release 129 (#223, #356, Oct 25-29). Some capabilities are also gated server-side per user: `CAPABILITY_DENIED` is a normal outcome.

## 2. Can we get accepted that fast?
- Developer Mode needs no approval: five taps on "App version" in Meta AI, then our sideloaded APK registers in minutes "without the need to submit it for publishing review"; one third-party app at a time, our own glasses only. Enough for the video. Listen (section 3) needs not even that: it is Bluetooth audio.
- Wearables Developer Center: a self-serve sign-up that creates a Managed Meta Account organization ("one organization per company", under its official name; nothing bars a two-person team); no approval step, fee or SLA is documented; new accounts hit a blank two-factor page in Sep-Oct (Meta: retry in a fresh browser session, keep building in Developer Mode; #172).
- Beta release channel: the permission justification is "for Meta's internal review only", yet no review gate shows (Meta, Oct 20-22: "No separate Voice Invocation approval is needed right now"); a version builds in 10-30 min; testers need a personal Meta account and must accept; changes can take over an hour to reach the glasses; some registrations fail attestation. Same day is possible, not reliable: half a day, only for voice launch.
- Public publishing: impossible before Nov 4 (no date), and the experimental and beta features stay unpublishable until Meta promotes them.
- Wording for the video and README. Listen: "the phone listens; the glasses are its earpiece, any Bluetooth earbuds work". Look: "demo build on Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit 1.0.1, running in Developer Mode on our own glasses; glasses apps cannot be published yet". Never "available on Ray-Ban Meta" or "approved by Meta".

## 3. Listening to a conversation
- (a) Glasses microphone over Bluetooth HFP (platform APIs, no DAT): mono, "16 kHz or 8 kHz (negotiated by the phone and glasses)"; beamforming "significantly reduces the volume of ambient sounds and other speakers. This is expected behavior" (DAT docs); a Gen 2 developer found people facing the wearer "barely audible or almost inaudible", which Meta called expected (#93); no dB or word-error figure exists. Android: `TYPE_BLUETOOTH_SCO` from `availableCommunicationDevices`, `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION`, `setCommunicationDevice()` (API 31+); iOS: `.playAndRecord` with `.allowBluetoothHFP`, `setPreferredInput`, wait about 2 s for the route. Costs: output drops to mono voice quality, media audio leaves the glasses (it may come out of the phone speaker, unverified), Meta AI sees a call, and any DAT session dies on release 129. Good for the senior's own question only.
- (b) DAT in-stream PCM (beta): the microphone array unbeamformed, ambient sound "at their natural levels", 16 kHz mono, timestamps about 200 ms behind the video (#347); reported on HSTN, Gen 2 and Display; development and beta channels only, never publishable today. It needs a camera stream (Meta's tip: LOW resolution, low frame rate; 2 fps is valid), so the LED is lit throughout and the battery drains ("eats battery", #318; no figure); no documented session cap (an 18-minute continuous camera session is reported on iOS 0.9.0, #260; a spring 3-minute cut was "addressed" in 0.6.0, #76). "While a camera stream is active, the glasses pause app audio" (Meta, #358; decided on the glasses, so presumably Android too), so advice cannot be whispered without stopping the stream, and restarting a stream "can fail" in 1.0.0 (#260).
- (c) The phone's own microphone (feasibility #9): omnidirectional, both voices at 0.5-1.5 m, `AudioRecord` 16 kHz mono PCM16, no LED, no DAT, nothing for Meta to approve; output stays on A2DP. It is live-calls.md route B on the senior's own phone: fine face to face, silent during a call on that same phone.

| Route | Doorstep pitch, other person at 1 m | Call on speaker | Whisper back | Publishable | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) HFP glasses mic | Senior clear, other voice barely audible | No: the call owns the audio, and the senior's phone mutes our capture | Mono over SCO | Yes | The senior's own question only |
| (b) In-stream PCM | Both voices, LED lit, battery drain | No: release 129 ends the session when it sees a call (inference) | Muted while streaming | No | Skip |
| (c) Phone mic | Both voices if the phone is in hand or on the table, not in a bag | Only when the call is on another phone (a landline on speaker); otherwise the second device of live-calls.md | A2DP stereo | Yes | Build |

- Continuous listening (route c): Android 14+ needs `RECORD_AUDIO`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE` and a `microphone` foreground service started while an activity is visible (other ways in: a notification action, a widget, a `VoiceInteractionService` app); a background start throws `SecurityException`; no time limit on that type; the green privacy dot shows. Muse Voice Transcribe realtime closes at 60 min (reconnect and merge). The glasses only play, so their battery (up to 8 h typical, Meta) and thermal state do not limit it. iOS would need the `audio` background mode, started in the foreground (unverified; there is no iOS companion).
- Speaking back: A2DP (44.1/48 kHz stereo) through the open-ear speakers, with the plain `TextToSpeech` on the media stream that `VerdictActivity` already uses (unverified on these glasses); under HFP the speech must go over SCO (`USAGE_VOICE_COMMUNICATION`, unverified) at phone-call quality. Keep each line under 3 s at moderate volume (how much a person at 1 m hears of the open-ear speakers is unmeasured) and send zeros while speaking so the transcript never hears Prudence.
- Hands-free "what do they want?" with zero DAT: a single temple tap sends play/pause to the phone's active media session (third-party guides, unverified), which a `MediaSession` in the listening service can turn into a check; the DAT alternative (an Inputs double tap) is beta.
- Latency budget, realtime (the phone streams to the live-calls.md relay): frame 0.1 s; phone to Fastify to Meta 0.05-0.2 s (unverified); partial transcript under 1 s (unmeasured); endpoint 0.5-1 s after the pause (unverified); detector about 0; Muse Spark 2.8 s at `minimal` or 4.7 s at `low` (measured once, `lib/muse.js`); push 0.05-0.2 s; speech start 0.1-0.5 s; A2DP 0.1-0.3 s (typical, unmeasured here). Rule cue ("They asked for a code: do not give it") 1-2 s after the words; Muse advice 4-7 s after the sentence. Tap-to-ask with the existing batch `/v1/asr/transcribe`: 3.3 s transcription and 5 s verdict measured on Oct 27 (`specs/check-something-else.md`), so 9-10 s after the tap.
- The claim for the video: "Prudence whispers its advice 4 to 7 seconds after the sentence" (realtime route at `minimal`); if only tap-to-ask ships, "within 10 seconds of her tap". Print the measured `latency_ms` on the card either way.

## 5. Rules and law for a listening coach
- Meta Wearables Acceptable Use Policy (last updated Sep 22, 2026, read at the source): "analyzing people in a user's surroundings" appears only as harassment, "to facilitate unwanted approaches or interactions"; judging a salesperson's words to protect the wearer is not that. What binds us: no "Surreptitiously collect recognizable audio or video content of the user or others", no "bypassing, tampering with, or obscuring" the capture LED, no "unique identification of people based on physical characteristics, such as voices and faces" (exceptions only "with prior Meta approval"), no sensors in "sensitive locations" (undefined). Meta's acceptable-use guide wants "Context on demand ... Deliberate, not ambient" and "narrow purpose, short retention, obvious to bystanders", so an always-on glasses listener would be questioned at review. The policy governs data obtained through the toolkit, which the phone-mic route never touches (the law still does). The Developer Terms also ban surveillance uses (Meta, #334).
- The capture LED is lit for any camera stream, audio-only use included, cannot be turned off, and covering it blocks capture. Route (c) has no light, so openness has to come from words and the phone screen.
- France, Code pénal art. 226-1 (version in force since 2024-03-23): capturing, recording or transmitting "paroles prononcées à titre privé ou confidentiel" without consent costs one year and €45,000; done "au vu et au su des intéressés sans qu'ils s'y soient opposés", their consent "est présumé". Whether a doorstep sales pitch is private speech at all is for case law (unverified).
- United States: federal one-party consent, 18 U.S.C. 2511(2)(d): the senior is "a party to the communication"; about a dozen states require all parties. Massachusetts, where Hadrien films: c. 272 §99 punishes secret interception by a device that hears or transmits, recording or not, without every party's authority (up to $10,000 and five years; civil damages of at least $100 a day or $1,000, plus punitive damages and fees), but it "only applies to secret recordings": once everyone knows, it is lawful (RCFP, DMLP; statute text unverified, malegislature.gov unreachable). "Nothing stored" is no defence there; saying it out loud is.
- EU AI Act: art. 5(1)(f) bans emotion inference only "in the areas of workplace and education institutions"; an emotion recognition system works "on the basis of their biometric data" (art. 3(39)), so judging the words of a transcript is not one, while analyzing the tone of a voice would be (art. 50(3) notice duties). Art. 50(1), from 2026-08-02: the senior must know she talks to an AI, which is evident. A senior using it privately is no "deployer" (art. 3(4)).
- GDPR: the other person's words are personal data; the senior's own use is household (art. 2(2)(c)), but recital 18 keeps the provider in scope; our basis is legitimate interest, "strictly necessary for the purposes of preventing fraud" (recital 47); nobody is identified, so no art. 9 biometric data.
- Defensible setup: the senior switches it on and says so ("an assistant is listening to help me; it records nothing"); the phone shows a large "Prudence is listening, nothing is recorded" banner (plus the LED if the camera runs); audio never stored, transcript in memory for the session only; the senior is a party to the conversation; nobody identified; words only, no tone or emotion analysis; stop when the other person objects. In the video every voice is a consenting actor.
- The line for the video: "Prudence listens only when Linda turns it on and says so out loud; nothing is recorded, and it never tries to identify anyone."

## 6. What the repo has, and the shortest build
- Server: `transcribeVoice({ wav })` in `lib/muse.js` (batch `/v1/asr/transcribe`, WAV, `PUSH_TO_TALK`, 30 s timeout; being moved behind `lib/llm` by the voice-tests session); `POST /api/check/voice` in `src/routes/mail.js` (browser-session auth, multipart WAV up to 8 MB, about 4 min at 16 kHz, judged as `voice_transcript`, audio never stored); `POST /api/devices/check` (device token, text only). No WebSocket, no `/api/listen`, no `@fastify/websocket` yet.
- Android companion: no `RECORD_AUDIO`, foreground service, `AudioManager`, Bluetooth, MediaProjection, `SpeechRecognizer` or `mwdat`. It has `TextToSpeech` in `VerdictActivity` (EN/FR, rate 0.9, media stream, so it already speaks in the glasses over A2DP), `ApiClient` (pair, check, ask, answers) and WorkManager; compileSdk and targetSdk 35, minSdk 26, Kotlin 2.1.0. DAT needs Android 10+ and Kotlin 2.2+: a 0.25-day bump before Look.
- Build 1, Listen, zero DAT (about 1.25 days): `POST /api/devices/check-voice` with the device token, reusing `transcribeVoice` and `runCheck` with a "live conversation" prompt variant (0.25 day); a `ListenService` (a `microphone` foreground service started from a big Listen button, `AudioRecord` 16 kHz mono into a 45-second ring buffer, zeroed while speaking), a "What do they want?" button plus the temple tap through `MediaSession`, WAV upload, the headline and the one action spoken, `VerdictActivity` opened (1 day). Streaming (rule cue in 1-2 s) adds 1.5 days on the live-calls.md relay: only if the web Listen route gets built anyway.
- Build 2, Look (2 days, earlier plan): DAT 1.0.1 core and camera, Mock Device Kit first, `stream.capturePhoto()` to the image check, the verdict spoken after the stream stops (app audio is paused during it), never SCO inside a session. Build 3, voice launch (1 day on a Beta channel): cut first.
- Day plan. Thu Oct 29: claim, server route, `ListenService` skeleton, glasses ordered (Listen needs only earbuds). Fri Oct 30: Listen end to end on a real Android phone with earbuds, tap-to-ear latency measured; Look on the Mock Device Kit; on the glasses: firmware, Developer Mode, A2DP speech and temple-tap checks. Sat Oct 31: Look on hardware if the glasses run it; doorstep rehearsal with actors (Hadrien, in Boston, needs glasses or earbuds and the Android phone). Sun Nov 1, hard cut at the dress rehearsal: a glasses scene not running end to end becomes the phone camera (Look) or plain earbuds (Listen). Nov 2-4: no new glasses work.
