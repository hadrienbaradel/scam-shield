# A public Prudence database: scam library, suspect list, scam watch (2026-11-04)

Vlad's idea (Nov 4): a public desktop site, not for seniors, with (1) a categorised library of real scam examples (email, SMS, chat, voice, letters) anyone can submit, bootstrapped from public data; (2) a public list of websites and phone numbers suspected of scams, open to submissions; (3) a watch that reads the internet and classifies big data breaches and new scam techniques. Four research passes behind this page, with every URL and accessed date: [corpora](public-database/corpora.md), [suspect lists and the legal side](public-database/suspect-lists.md), [watch sources and social platforms](public-database/watch-sources.md), [taxonomies](public-database/taxonomy.md). Not legal advice. Decision proposal: `_product/specs/public-database.md`.

## Findings in eight lines

1. The gap is real. No openly licensed, categorised, multi-channel library of scam messages with a submission channel exists; the only message-text repo with PR intake has 360 seed texts and 0 stars. No one pairs a number or site with the message that used it under an open licence. No one publishes an AI-read public watch; the field ranks breaches and alerts by hand.
2. Bootstrapping is lawful from a short list: the Zenodo 8339691 bundle and the Nazario corpus (CC BY 4.0, email), UCI SMS Spam and the Mishra-Soni smishing set (CC BY 4.0), two romance-scam chat corpora from Granada (CC BY 4.0, about 900k words), the NCSU robocall set (1,432 real calls with transcripts, public domain), FTC and IC3 text (US public domain), cybermalveillance.gouv.fr (Licence Ouverte 2.0), NCSC (OGL v3), Scamwatch (CC BY). Avoid share-alike strings (the Kaggle bundle, CLAIR) and non-commercial sets (phishing_pot, Smishtank); never YouTube scam-baiter transcripts, Singapore ScamShield, Action Fraud, Signal-Arnaques or BBB pages.
3. Every real corpus carries personal data (Enron mail, SpamAssassin headers, Nazario's later mailboxes, phone numbers in the SMS sets, unredacted romance chats). A scammer's number or address is personal data under GDPR too. Publishing needs a redaction pass whatever the licence.
4. Suspect lists: nothing free and redistributable pairs numbers or sites with evidence. Reusable without a contract: FTC Do Not Call and FCC Unwanted Calls complaint data (public domain, numbers and a subject, explicitly unverified), Phishing.Database (MIT, domains only). Every phone-reputation site forbids reuse (tellows, Truecaller, 800notes, Should I Answer); France has no open number-level data at all (33700 feeds the operators only).
5. The legal exposure sits in part 2. Naming a number or site as a scam is lawful in principle in France (legitimate interest plus freedom of expression; Signal-Arnaques won on appeal in 2023), but every lost case (CA Montpellier 2012, CA Reims 2018, T. com. Paris 2019 and 2021) turns on one thing: a precise notice left unanswered. Our own label ("scam", a score) is our content, outside both LCEN host immunity and US Section 230; user reports are covered, our verdicts are not. The DSA's notice-and-action articles (11-14, 16-18) apply even to a micro enterprise. Spoofed caller IDs mean a listed number can be a victim's.
6. Community lists that survive have a trusted feeder plus named reviewers (MetaMask eth-phishing-detect: bot PRs, CODEOWNERS, three-strikes false-positive policy) or a vote score with decay instead of a queue (PhoneBlock). Solo lists with open intake die under their issue backlog (blocklistproject archived Oct 2026 with 261 open issues; scamsniffer, 265 unanswered). Evidence per entry lives nowhere today.
7. Watch sources: about fifteen feeds work today and may be summarised. Breaches: Have I Been Pwned's breach list (JSON, no key, CC BY 4.0, with count, data classes, dates and a verified flag), ransomware.live, the California attorney general's CSV. Techniques: IC3 PSA and CSA, CISA, NCSC, Europol (ask before reuse), cybermalveillance (three more Atom feeds than we read), Krebs, BleepingComputer, The Record, Malwarebytes Labs, Google Threat Intelligence, Which?. No FTC consumer-alerts RSS exists. Social: X is pay-per-use only and bans scraping, LinkedIn has no read API and bans scraping, Reddit's RSS ends on Nov 13, 2026 and its public API in March 2027; Bluesky (open API and firehose), Mastodon hashtag timelines and the YouTube Data API (titles only) are fine.
8. Taxonomy: no public one is both senior-readable and coded. Anchor the structure on the Federal Reserve ScamClassifier (two categories, nine impostor-centric types), own a user-facing layer of 20 families (delivery, bank, government, tech-support, family-emergency, new-number, romance, investment, prize, charity, job, rental, renewal, toll-fine, utility, account, sextortion, invoice, marketplace, health; recovery as a 21st), keep channel as a separate tag, and cross-reference each family to IC3 (the only yearly 60+ losses per type), the FTC Imposter sub-categories and the cybermalveillance fiche slugs. 2025, people 60+: 201,266 IC3 complaints and $7.75B lost (+59%); the FTC counts 469,597 reports and over $3B.

## What this means for Prudence

- Part 1 (the library) is low risk, high value and empty of competitors: it is also our evaluation set, the few-shot pool for Muse Spark, the Learn tab's raw material and the public proof of the "world context" next to the senior's "life context".
- Part 3 (the watch) extends built code: `src/jobs/threatfeed.js` already turns IC3 and cybermalveillance alerts into candidate cards that a human approves on `/admin/#/cards`. The new work is the source list, a classification schema, the breach side (HIBP) and a public page for approved items. It also answers Breach exposure's "source of breach news" question.
- Part 2 (the suspect list) is the crowded, legally exposed one. Reshape it: indicators (domains, sender addresses, numbers) derived from library entries that carry evidence, shown as "reported N times, last seen", with decay, a public removal path answered within days, and never a private person's number as a stand-alone claim. Crowd intake of bare numbers is what the dead lists did.
- Intake that survives: the Prudence app itself as the trusted feeder ("Share this with the library" under a verdict, opt-in per message, redacted; the same tap as the Report upstream idea), GitHub issue forms and pull requests for everyone else (free moderation, provenance, no new infrastructure), Muse Spark as the first reviewer, a human batch once a week.
- Licence: CC BY 4.0 for everything published (compatible with every source we may use, the licence HIBP chose; attribution keeps provenance). ODbL is the share-alike alternative for the indicator list if we want derivatives to stay open.
- Shape: the repo is the database (JSON per entry, a schema, CI checks), a static site built from it, an API later. One scheduled job (the watch) and one moderation page (the existing admin console) are the only moving parts.
- Timing: nothing here fits the video due tonight. First week after the deadline: library v0 (two to three days), watch v0 (two days), suspect list v0 (two days). Costs that do not go away: a weekly human review, answering notices, a paid HIBP key only if we look up the senior's own email (the public breach list is free).

## Open questions

- Who is the operator in the legal sense (a person, an association, a company)? The DSA contact point and the removal process name someone.
- Numbers at all in v1, or only domains and sender addresses until a notice process exists?
- Name and domain of the public site; GitHub Pages or the OVH server.
- Who reviews weekly after the hackathon (already open for the pattern cards)?
- Verified at the source but worth a second look before building: Scamwatch and Action Fraud licence wording (both pages blocked bots; taken from snippets), Smishtank's dataset licence (page 404), Europol's current legal notice, PhishStats terms (502).
