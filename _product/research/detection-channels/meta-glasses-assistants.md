# Meta glasses and the assistants seniors already own
What Scam Shield can run on Ray-Ban Meta glasses and on Alexa, Siri, Gemini, Bixby and smart displays before Nov 4: access, SDK, hardware, lead times, days, demo fit. Written 2026-10-28.

## Decision
Build the glasses "look at the letter" loop on the Mock Device Kit on Oct 29 (no cost, reusable for the phone-camera scene) and spend the hardware day only if a Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 or Oakley Meta HSTN is in hand by Thursday Oct 30 and the full loop (photo from the glasses, verdict spoken in the ear) runs that same day; hard cut at the Nov 1 dress rehearsal, after which the phone camera films the same scene. Reason: the scene lifts Demo (+1 to +2) and Impact (+0.5) but not Meta tooling (0 unless the narration names Muse Spark reading the frame and Muse Voice Transcribe hearing the question), costs 2 of the CTO's 6 remaining days (3 with voice launch), and depends on $379-449 hardware with unverified delivery, undocumented latency and a beta photo path. The cheapest hands-free "ask" is a Siri dictation shortcut (0.5 day, no app, no hardware), then an Alexa custom skill (1 day, an Echo, fr-FR supported): both speak the verdict on a device the senior already owns, which is the stronger Impact story. Meta AI Connectors is an application only; Gemini AppFunctions, Bixby and Nest Hub are closed to a new app this year.

## Build sheet
- **Glasses, status and access.** Wearables Device Access Toolkit (DAT) 1.0.0 on Maven Central and GitHub 2026-09-24 (six experimental features), 1.0.1 on 2026-10-09 (HEVC corruption and stream jitter fixes); first release 0.1.0 on 2025-10-30. Meta's FAQ: the developer preview "has ended", rollout from 2026-09-30, features arrive on a rolling basis; both GitHub READMEs still say "developer preview" and that audio streaming and standalone photo capture are "unavailable for production publishing". Free: no fee, waitlist or approval on any page; using it means agreeing to the Wearables Developer Terms and the Acceptable Use Policy. SDK worldwide, full feature set only in the 17 AI-glasses countries (Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, UK, US); Display and Neural Band: Canada, France, Germany, Italy, UK, US. Boston and France both qualify.
- **Developer Mode (zero lead time, no account).** Meta AI app > Settings > App Info > tap "App version" five times > switch on Developer Mode > Enable; the app then appears under Meta AI settings > App connections > Developer mode apps. Manifest placeholders `APPLICATION_ID` and `CLIENT_TOKEN` are both `"0"`. Only one third-party app stays registered (a new registration replaces the previous one); the mode is tied to each linked device and can be cleared by app or firmware updates; without it devices never become eligible, permission requests fail and `createSession` fails.
- **Wearables Developer Center (only for voice launch and invited testers).** Sign-up at https://wearables.developer.meta.com/signup/landing/ creates a Managed Meta Account (MMA) organization on work.meta.com (one admin, the company's formal name, invites per contributor; no verification step, cost, approval time or country rule described). Project: name, description, icon PNG/JPEG at most 200×200; the Permissions tab (camera, microphone, voice invocation) needs a written justification "for Meta's internal review only"; each version (its own on-glasses DAT app) builds in about 10 minutes, up to 30. One private "Beta" release channel per integration carrying one version; testers are invited by email, need a personal Meta account (MMA not supported, sign up at https://auth.meta.com/), accept, then pick the channel under device menu > Settings > Release Channel in the Meta AI app; the tester cap shows on the channel's Users tab, preview-era pages said "as many as 100 testers" (unverified for 1.0).
- **Platforms and artifacts.** Android 10+, Android Studio Flamingo+, Kotlin 2.2+; iOS 17.2+, Xcode 26.4+. Maven Central group `com.meta.wearable`, version 1.0.1, no credentials or extra repository: `mwdat-core`, `mwdat-camera`, `mwdat-display`, `mwdat-inputs`, `mwdat-motion`, `mwdat-mockdevice`, `mwdat-speech`; manifest meta-data `com.meta.wearable.mwdat.APPLICATION_ID`. iOS: Swift Package https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios (1.0.1). Telemetry and crash reporting on by default (manifest opt-outs `ANALYTICS_OPT_OUT`, `CRASH_REPORTING_OPT_OUT`); "Meta may also collect data on how users' devices interact with your app".
- **Glasses and firmware.** Supported: Ray-Ban Meta Gen 1 and Gen 2, Meta Ray-Ban Display, Oakley Meta HSTN, Oakley Meta Vanguard, "newer SKUs of these lines are covered as they launch", capability varies by device; the docs also list Ray-Ban Meta Optics and "Meta glasses" (CHANGELOG: Display 0.4.0, Optics 0.6.0, Meta Glasses 0.8.0 on 2026-06-25); Gen 3 and Ray-Ban Meta Audio not listed yet (unverified). SDK 1.0.1 pairs with Meta AI app V292 and glasses firmware V128 on every listed model (1.0.0: V290 / V128); registration started from the Meta AI app needs SDK 1.0.0 or later. Check the firmware: Meta AI app > Devices > glasses > gear > General > About > Version. Pairing only through the Meta AI app; registration then per-device permission (`Wearables.RequestPermissionContract()`, "Allow once" or "Allow always"; camera granted at app level, confirmed per device). Some features are limited server-side per user: `DeviceSessionError.CAPABILITY_DENIED` is routine, `INSUFFICIENT_SDK_VERSION` is terminal. Capture LED: a white blink plus a sound on a photo, lit for a whole video, cannot be disabled, covering it blocks capture, and the camera shuts off on tamper (July 2026 firmware, thousands of units, under 0.1% of users (unverified)); the DAT docs never mention the LED.
- **Camera API.** `StreamConfiguration(videoQuality, frameRate, compressVideo)`: `VideoQuality` HIGH 720×1280, MEDIUM 504×896, LOW 360×640; 2, 7, 15, 24 or 30 fps; decoded YUV by default, HEVC with `compressVideo = true`; Bluetooth Classic, auto step-down by one resolution step then frame rate (30 to 24), never below 15 fps; per-frame compression lowers quality and lower settings can look better; no latency, time-to-first-frame, battery or duration figures anywhere. `camera.stream.capturePhoto()` only while streaming, returns `PhotoData.Bitmap` or `PhotoData.HEIC`, one capture in flight (iOS: wearer-taken photos share one publisher with yours): the non-experimental path. Standalone photo (beta, unpublishable, "less reliable than the video stream"): resolution SMALL (480p-class), MEDIUM (720p-class, default), LARGE (1080p-class), FULL 4032×3024; quality LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH; JPEG or HEIC as the glasses produce; transfer progress as a fraction; 30 s of total silence fails the capture; stop the stream first; needs toolkit 1.0.0+ and the CAMERA permission; `SETUP_FAILED` at startup is the common failure, retry once; `DEVICE_HEALTH_CRITICAL` is a capture error. A session pauses or stops when another experience takes the device, the glasses are removed or folded, Bluetooth drops or the app unregisters; STOPPED is terminal; background execution is undocumented (the 0.9.0 sample records "even when backgrounded"). `Device` exposes battery, charging, wear, hinge and thermal state. Latency estimate (unsourced): a few seconds to open a session and stream, about a second per 720p frame or in-stream photo, more for a FULL 12 MP transfer, plus Muse Spark about 4.7 s at low effort (feasibility #12): "under ten seconds from look to verdict" is plausible, "within seconds" is not until measured.
- **Microphone and speaker.** (a) Bluetooth HFP through platform APIs: mono, 16 or 8 kHz, beamformed to the wearer (other speakers come through much quieter, by design), switches the glasses away from A2DP; Android `AudioManager`, pick `TYPE_BLUETOOTH_SCO` from `availableCommunicationDevices`, `MODE_IN_COMMUNICATION`, `setCommunicationDevice()` (API 31+). (b) In-stream PCM (beta, not in production channels, API may change): 16-bit PCM at 16,000 / 44,100 / 48,000 Hz, not beamformed, playback stays on A2DP; `AudioCodec.PCM`, `AudioSampleRate.RATE_16000`, one channel. (c) On-device transcription (experimental): `addSpeech`, flow `transcriptions` (partial and final), DAT microphone permission, languages undocumented. Speaker: plain A2DP, stereo, 44.1 or 48 kHz, listed uses "Music, media playback, TTS", no toolkit API; iOS: a playback session routes to the glasses automatically (verify the active route); Android `TextToSpeech` on the media stream should reach the glasses as the connected A2DP device (unverified on these glasses); a community DAT 1.0 app reads answers back through the glasses (unverified).
- **Voice launch.** The only documented phrase is "Hey Meta, start {YOUR APP NAME}"; Meta AI launches or foregrounds the app (cold launch if needed, no running session required) and delivers a `LaunchApp` invocation the app must answer exactly once or Meta AI may show a fallback UI; the name in the WDC configuration is the matched keyword; whether invocations arrive "is determined entirely by your Developer Center configuration"; a new version includes the Voice Invocation permission; avoid "AI" in the name; an active WebMCP web app on the glasses takes precedence; no languages listed; nothing passes the rest of the utterance; experimental in 1.0.0. Community (unverified): the phrase in use is "Hey Meta, open <name>" ("Ehi Meta, apri"); in Developer Mode the tile and button work but the trigger may fail to arrive; hands-free needs the WDC route (signed build, own account, Developer Mode off); allow about 15 minutes linked before testing; avoid names Meta AI handles itself (music, camera, WhatsApp); "temporarily granted to every app" is relayed Meta staff talk. Inputs (buttons, captouch) are experimental and approval-gated in WDC. No on-glasses apps; the wake word cannot be hooked and Meta AI accepts no third-party commands (community and press, unverified). Display only: `session.addDisplay()` then `display.sendContent { flexBox / text / button / video }`, each call replaces the whole display; Web Apps (HTML/JS in the glasses' Chromium browser) are Display-only; the Mock Device Kit has no display support.
- **Android integration steps.** Gradle `com.meta.wearable:mwdat-core`, `mwdat-camera` (plus `mwdat-speech`, `mwdat-mockdevice`) 1.0.1 from `mavenCentral()`; permissions `BLUETOOTH`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`, `INTERNET`; `manifestPlaceholders` `mwdat_application_id` / `mwdat_client_token` (`"0"`); an activity intent filter with a custom URL scheme for the Meta AI callback; `Wearables.initialize(context)` in `Application.onCreate`; `Wearables.startRegistration(activity)`; wait for `RegistrationState.REGISTERED`; `Wearables.createSession(AutoDeviceSelector())`; `session.start()`; add capabilities only at STARTED; `addCamera` > `camera.stream.start()` > `capturePhoto()`; teardown `camera.stop()` then `session.stop()`. Copy from the CameraAccess sample (Debug icon > Pair RayBan Meta > PowerOn, Unfold, Don > pick media > stream). Mock Device Kit: simulates registration, permissions, power/fold/don states, feeds an H.265 file (e.g. 540×960) or the phone camera as the glasses camera, `setCapturedImage(Uri)` for photos. Meta ships Claude Code, Codex and Muse Code plugins (experimental), the Wearables MCP server `https://mcp.developer.meta.com/wearables` (tool `search_dat_docs`, no auth) and `AGENTS.md` in each repo. Reference build: hey-casa (Android, DAT 1.0, Developer Mode, voice in, HTTP call, answer spoken through the glasses) (unverified).

## Corrections to feasibility.md
