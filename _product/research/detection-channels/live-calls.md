# Live calls: judging a phone conversation while it happens
Answers how a live call can be judged while it happens (which client, which transcription, what latency, what it needs, how long, whether it fits before Nov 4), written 2026-10-28; technical only, the recording law stays in `_product/research/feasibility.md` #10.

## Decision
Build the web route (A) for the Nov 4 video and for real seniors after it: the senior puts the phone on speaker and taps Listen on a laptop (Chrome) or iPad (Safari) next to it; the browser streams 16 kHz PCM through a Fastify WebSocket to Muse Voice Transcribe realtime, the detector fires a rule cue within 1-2 s of the ask and Muse Spark explains 4-7 s after the sentence, spoken by the browser. It is 2-3 days, every call is a real Meta call, and it works in English and French. The senior's own phone is not an option on Android or iOS (the OS silences or suspends a third-party app during a call), so the second device is the honest product too. The Twilio public-number fork (E) is the only route with no tap per call and stays an optional day-6 scene with a US number; the browser's Web Speech API (A2) is the half-day fallback.

## Build sheet
Muse Voice Transcribe realtime (the STT behind A, B, D and E)
- Endpoint `wss://api.meta.ai/v1/asr/realtime` (optional `?sessionId=`). The HTTP `Authorization` header is ignored: the first text frame, within 10 s, is the JSON handshake `{authorization:{accessToken:"Bearer <MODEL_API_KEY>"}, audioEncoding:"PCM_16KHZ"|"PCM_24KHZ" (required), model:"muse-voice-transcribe-1.0", mode:"PUSH_TO_TALK" (default)|"ENDPOINTING"|"DIARIZATION", partialMode:"CUMULATIVE" (default)|"DELTA", emitAudioProgress (default true), keywords[], languageBias[] (omit to auto-detect)}`; settings lock once the handshake succeeds. The key travels in the frame, so the browser never opens this socket: Fastify relays.
- First server frame: `{sessionId}`, the only frame without `type`; hold audio until it arrives; log `sessionId` (Meta's only support handle).
- Audio: binary frames of raw signed 16-bit little-endian mono PCM, no container, no base64. 24 kHz is the engine's native rate (48,000 bytes/s); 16 kHz (32,000 bytes/s) is resampled server-side and is right for a phone speaker heard by a tablet. Pace at real time: the official sample sends 80 ms frames (3,840 bytes at 24 kHz); at 16 kHz send 2,560 bytes per 80 ms or 3,200 bytes per 100 ms.
- Close codes: no handshake within 10 s, an audio backlog above 5 s, or input below real time for 10 s close with 1008 ("pad gaps with silence"), so mute and pauses are zero-filled frames, never a gap, and a background tab with throttled timers trips it; 60 minutes close with 1011 "Max session duration reached" (no resume: reconnect and merge transcripts); 1013 rate limited (back off); 1011 internal failure (retry after a delay); 1000 normal finish after the final result. Idle input or stalled output also closes the session (durations undocumented).
- Events (JSON text frames, route on `type`, ignore unknown): `transcript` {transcript, final, audioProcessedMs} (partials, cumulative by default, no turnId: captions and detector input); `speechStart` and `speechEnd` {turnId}; `speechComplete` {turnId, transcript} (the finished, punctuated turn: detector plus Muse Spark); `speaker` {label} (DIARIZATION only: labels A, B, session-scoped, "not tuned for low-latency"); `audioProgress`; `error` (fatal). Turn detection is server-side, no VAD thresholds or silence timeouts exposed; `{"type":"endStream"}` ends the whole session, then wait for close 1000. Use ENDPOINTING for live advice and let Muse Spark infer who said what.
- Languages: 25 auto-detected including English and French (`languageBias: ["english","french"]` nudges without forcing); turn-level timestamps only; no TTS or speech-to-speech on the Model API, so the spoken advice comes from browser `speechSynthesis` or Android `TextToSpeech`. Muse Spark 1.3 takes `input_audio` (MP3/WAV) but audio "is not fully supported" (Meta says use Spark 1.2 or Voice Transcribe).
- Price and limits: $0.18 per hour of audio, metered in whole seconds, streaming and file priced the same, ZDR at parity (enablement undocumented), no Contributor mode; no RPM or TPM caps; 256 concurrent streams and 16,000 streams per hour per team, shared with file transcription, rejected streams close with 1013. A 10-minute call is $0.03 of STT; Muse Spark checks dominate at about $0.013 each (10-20 per call: $0.13-0.26). Standard mode: content not used for training; no country restriction listed, access from France unverified.
- Batch fallback for a clip: `POST https://api.meta.ai/v1/asr/transcribe`, `Authorization: Bearer`, multipart `request` JSON plus `audio` WAV (mono, 16-bit, 16 or 24 kHz), at most 10 minutes (400) and 32 MB (413), 429 on concurrency, returns `transcript` and `turns[]` with startMs/endMs; `Accept: text/event-stream` streams the same events.
- Node: Node 22 ships a global `WebSocket` client (verified locally); `@fastify/websocket` 11.3.4 serves the browser socket; neither is in `package.json` today; Meta samples are Python and curl only (Pipecat has a Python service (unverified)). No official latency figure; press cites 80 ms chunks and a per-word adaptive delay (unverified).

A. Web route (recommended)
- Browser: `getUserMedia({audio:{echoCancellation:true, noiseSuppression:true, autoGainControl:true}})` in a foreground tab on a secure origin (HTTPS or `localhost`, otherwise `navigator.mediaDevices` is undefined; errors NotAllowedError, NotReadableError); `navigator.wakeLock.request("screen")` (Chrome 84+, Safari 16.4+ (unverified)); an `AudioWorklet` (Safari 14.1+; Chrome 66+ (unverified)) that decimates the 44.1 or 48 kHz Float32 stream to 16 kHz Int16 (write the manual resampler: Safari honouring `new AudioContext({sampleRate:16000})` is unverified) and posts 100 ms buffers; `WebSocket("wss://<tunnel>/api/listen?token=...")` sending binary frames. Skip `MediaRecorder`: WebM/MP4 chunks after the first are not independently decodable and Safari records `audio/mp4` AAC (unverified).
- Server: `@fastify/websocket` route `/api/listen` authenticated by session cookie or device token, one Meta socket per client, frames relayed at wall-clock pace, `lib/detector.js` on every partial, `museChat` with a "live conversation" prompt variant (new channel value in `lib/schema.js`) on every `speechComplete` with the last ~6 turns, the senior's life context and the detector facts, only while no verdict is pending (coalesce turns); a detector hit on codes, gift cards, transfers or remote access escalates to a red card without waiting for Muse; 20 s deadline then detector-only `degraded`. Events back: `caption` (partials), `cue` (rule hit), `advice` (headline, one action, one "say this" line), spoken with `speechSynthesis` (already in `public/js/ui.js`); send zeros while speaking or accept echo cancellation. Nothing stored: the transcript lives in memory for the session. A mock STT provider for `make test`; `META_MODEL_API_KEY` reused through `make secrets-set`.
- Why not the senior's own phone: iOS deactivates every app's audio session when a call arrives and suspends the app if the call is accepted; Safari and WKWebView mute capture in the background (WebKit says "behaving correctly") and Home Screen web apps have broken-capture reports (2024); on Android, Chrome is an ordinary app and reads silence during a call (see C).
- Latency per spoken sentence: microphone to 100 ms frame 0.1 s; browser to Fastify to Meta through the tunnel 0.05-0.2 s (unverified); partial transcript behind speech under 1 s (unverified); endpointer closes the turn 0.5-1 s after the pause (unverified); detector ~0 s; Muse Spark 2.8 s at `minimal` (~280 reasoning tokens) or 4.7 s at `low` (~930), one measured case, default 11.5 s is too slow; push and `speechSynthesis` start 0.1-0.5 s (unverified). Sum: rule cue on screen and spoken 1-2 s after the ask ("They asked for a code. Hang up."); Muse explanation 4-7 s after the sentence. Keep the spoken line under 3 s and print the measured `latency_ms` on the card.
- Senior steps: 1) the call feels off: tap the speaker icon on the phone; 2) on the laptop or tablet left open on the Listen page, tap the one 72 px Listen button (microphone permission granted once at setup by the family); 3) say "my Scam Shield is listening" (openness, feasibility #10); 4) keep the phone a hand's width (20-30 cm) from the device; 5) do what the card says: hang up or read the "say this" line; 6) optionally "Ask my trusted person" (existing guardian loop). Stop, or the 60-minute cap, ends the session.
- Effort 2-3 days: relay plus detector and Muse hook ~1, Listen screen ~1, prompt tuning, tests, tunnel WSS and rehearsal 0.5-1. Chrome on a laptop is the most robust target, iPad Safari in a plain tab second (not the Home Screen app until tested). Far-field audio at 20-30 cm through room echo is less accurate than a headset: rehearse the scripted call twice. Card footer: "Transcribed by Muse Voice Transcribe, judged by Muse Spark 1.3, nothing recorded". Pre-recording checks: `GET https://api.meta.ai/v1/status`, one realtime handshake, the tunnel's `wss://`, microphone permission.
- Fallback A2: Web Speech API (`SpeechRecognition` with `continuous`, `interimResults`, `lang: "fr-FR"` or `en-US`) posting final sentences to the existing `/api/check`: ~0.5 day once A's Listen screen exists (about 1 day standalone, inference). Chrome sends the audio to Google, Safari uses the Siri engine (Siri must be enabled), Firefox disabled by default; it auto-stops after silence (restart loop); `processLocally` is experimental (`SpeechRecognition.available()`, `install()`; Edge lists six on-device locales, none French) and not for this week; it loses the Muse Voice Transcribe credit on screen.

B. Android companion on a second device, and C. why the senior's own phone cannot
- C: since Android 10, while `AudioManager.getMode()` is `MODE_IN_CALL` the call receives the audio and every ordinary app's capture is muted (silence), speakerphone or not: the mute is per client in the audio server, not acoustic. Exceptions: an accessibility service with its UI on top (`VOICE_RECOGNITION` or `HOTWORD` source) or a pre-installed app with `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT` (`signature|privileged|role`, "not for use by third-party applications"); the `VOICE_CALL`, `VOICE_UPLINK`, `VOICE_DOWNLINK` sources need the same permission. Android 16 blocks granting accessibility permissions, sideloading from a browser or messaging app and disabling Play Protect during a call with a non-contact (Play services v26.41, 2026-10-19, back-ports the accessibility block to Android 15 and earlier); Android 17 Advanced Protection limits AccessibilityService to verified accessibility tools. The accessibility trick is the pattern Google is shutting down: not a product or demo path. `AudioRecord` starts without error and reads zeros (inference); no 2025-26 Pixel or Samsung report found (unverified). A VoIP call owned by our own app could be captured, but scam calls arrive on the cellular line.
- B: `RECORD_AUDIO` plus `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE`, a service with `foregroundServiceType="microphone"` started while an activity is visible or from a notification action (Android 14+ throws `SecurityException` from the background; the 6-hour timeout does not apply to `microphone`), `AudioRecord(VOICE_RECOGNITION, 16000, MONO, PCM_16BIT)`, 100 ms chunks over an OkHttp WebSocket to `/api/listen`, the existing `VerdictActivity` plus `TextToSpeech`. The companion today declares `RECEIVE_SMS`, `INTERNET`, `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` only, compileSdk and targetSdk 35, minSdk 26, no OkHttp. ~0.5 day service, ~1 day screen, pairing and TTS, ~0.5 day device testing: 1.5-2 days after A's server, for the same picture as A plus a sideload scene.
- `SpeechRecognizer` is "not intended to be used for continuous recognition": it stops at each silence and must be re-armed; on-device through `createOnDeviceSpeechRecognizer` (API 31) and `checkRecognitionSupport` (API 33), French offline unverified until tested; it hears the same silence during a call.
- `CallScreeningService` (feasibility #8) keeps a role on the senior's own phone without audio: on an unknown number post "Put the call on speaker and tap Listen on your tablet", and a post-call "what did they say?" flow.

D and E. Twilio routes
- Prices, US (August 2026): local number $1.15/month, inbound local $0.0085/min, outbound US/Canada $0.0140/min, Media Streams $0.0044/min, ConversationRelay $0.07/min, conference from $0.0018/participant/min, recording $0.0025/min, transcription $0.05/min. France: geographic number (01-05) $1.35/month, inbound $0.0100/min, outbound to French mobiles $0.1603/min ($0.0404 from the EEA), to landlines $0.0187/min, Media Streams $0.0044/min, ConversationRelay $0.07/min; national (09), mobile and toll-free prices not shown (unverified).
- Account: a trial calls only up to 5 verified numbers in the sign-up country, expires 30 days after sign-up, 75 voice minutes, shared trial numbers, some TwiML verbs blocked, a spoken trial notice (wording unverified); Media Streams and ConversationRelay are listed under post-upgrade allocations, so upgrade (hours) and buy a US local number (instant). A French number needs a regulatory bundle: an address in France (no PO box), government ID plus proof of address for an individual or K-bis plus SIREN for a business, review time unstated (days (unverified)); local and national numbers prohibit sales or telemarketing and emergency use; toll-free is not available to individuals.
- Media Streams: unidirectional `<Start><Stream url="wss://<tunnel>/twilio/stream" track="both_tracks"/>` does not block TwiML (`inbound_track`, `outbound_track` or `both_tracks`, which counts as two of the four tracks per call), followed by `<Pause length="3600"/>` (D) or `<Dial callerId="<scam shield number>">` to the senior's real phone (E); bidirectional `<Connect><Stream>` blocks TwiML and receives the inbound track only. Messages from Twilio: `connected`, `start` (`mediaFormat` always `audio/x-mulaw`, 8000 Hz, 1 channel), `media` (`payload` base64 raw mu-law, `chunk` from 1, `timestamp` in ms; about 20 ms and 160 bytes per chunk (unverified)), `dtmf`, `stop`, `mark`; the server sends `media`, `mark`, `clear` (bidirectional only). WSS on 443, validate `X-Twilio-Signature`, regions US1, IE1, AU1. The server decodes mu-law to PCM16, upsamples 8 to 16 kHz (repeat or linear interpolation) and feeds the same STT pipeline; advice goes to the senior's web app over SSE.
- ConversationRelay: `<Connect><ConversationRelay url="wss://..."/>`, `transcriptionProvider` Google or Deepgram (default Deepgram `nova-3-general`; Deepgram lists `fr` and `fr-CA`), `ttsProvider` Google, Amazon or ElevenLabs, `language` default `en-US`, `transcriptionLanguage` and `ttsLanguage` overrides, `multi` auto-detect (Deepgram plus ElevenLabs only), `speechTimeout` 600-5000 ms or `auto`; no language table and no latency figure; requires accepting the "Predictive and Generative AI/ML Features Addendum". Speaking advice into the call means the scammer hears it: a design choice.
- Merge steps (D): iPhone: More, then Add Call (Add People on newer iOS), dial the Scam Shield number, tap Merge Calls; up to five participants on GSM, carrier limits apply; "You can't merge calls if the second call was incoming"; ending the second or merged call ends both; conferencing may be unavailable on VoLTE or Wi-Fi Calling (search summaries (unverified)). Android Google Phone: hold, place the second call, tap Call merge (unverified). A contact or Shortcut can pre-dial the number but cannot press Merge.
- Forwarding (E): the senior gives out the Scam Shield number or forwards the line to it (`**21*<number>#` to activate, `##21#` to cancel, 3GPP MMI codes, not fetched (unverified)); Twilio forks the audio and dials the real phone, so every call is judged with no action. Cost US about $0.027/min (0.0085 + 0.0140 + 0.0044) plus $1.15/month; France about $0.175/min with mobile termination ($0.055 from the EEA) plus $1.35/month; some plans bill forwarded minutes (unverified). It breaks caller ID (Twilio passes a caller ID only for numbers you own or verified (unverified)), bypasses the phone's unknown-caller screening, forwards family calls too, forwards no SMS, moves voicemail to Twilio, and French local and national numbers prohibit emergency use.
- Effort: upgrade and US number (hours), TwiML app and signature validation (0.5 day), stream decoder and pipeline adapter (0.5), two-phone test over the tunnel (0.5), optional ConversationRelay voice-back (0.5-1): 1.5-2.5 days after A's server. A French number this week is paperwork risk: film the French scene with the web route.

F. What the phones ship natively (October 2026) and what is left
- Google Phone Scam Detection: Pixel 6+ in the US, Pixel 9+ in Australia, Canada, France, Germany, India, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Singapore, Spain and the UK; SIM and location gated; off by default; never on calls with contacts; Gemini Nano on-device, nothing stored or sent; a beep for both parties at the start and every few minutes; no language list published. Expanding to Galaxy S26 in the US (Samsung AI Scam detection needs Android 16 with One UI 8.5; delivered through Android CallCore (unverified)). Google flags over 5 million suspected scam calls and texts a month; Messages scam detection covers French.
- Apple iOS 26: Call Screening asks unknown callers for a name and reason (Never / Ask Reason for Calling / Silence; off while roaming and for 24 h after an emergency call; contacts never screened; default state unstated (unverified)) in English and French among others; Live Voicemail transcribes in 26 locales including French (France); call recording in 32 languages but transcription in 15 locales with no French; Messages quarantines unknown senders.
- Carriers: T-Mobile Scam ID and Scam Block (free, Premium $4/line (unverified)), AT&T ActiveArmor (free, "may inadvertently block wanted calls"), Verizon Call Filter (free, Plus $3.99/line (unverified)). France: Orange Téléphone labels numbers (indésirable, démarchage, approuvé); ARCEP's number authentication cuts unauthenticated French numbers since October 2024 and masks foreign-origin French mobile numbers since 1 January 2026, yet spoofing reports rose from 531 (2023) to over 19,000 (2025); reserved telemarketing prefixes 0162, 0163, 0270, 0271, 0377, 0378, 0424, 0425, 0568, 0569, 0948, 0949.
- The gap: every native tool answers "who is calling" or "is this number genuine"; none judges what is said against the senior's own life, explains in plain words, coaches during the call or alerts a trusted person, and none has a third-party API. Say on screen that they exist (off by default or unknown callers only) and that Scam Shield is the friend at the table.

## Gaps
- Muse Voice Transcribe latency for partials and for `speechComplete` after a pause: measure on day 1 with a scripted scam call, then print `latency_ms` on the card.
- Far-field accuracy of Muse Voice Transcribe on a phone speaker at 20-30 cm, in French and English, through room echo.
- Meta API reachability from France (the Geographic Use Policy page was not retrievable); ZDR enablement and Standard-mode retention.
- Safari: whether `AudioContext({sampleRate:16000})` is honoured, the `MediaRecorder` MIME list, iPad Home Screen web app microphone; Chrome Android `continuous` quirks.
- Android second device: a 10-minute RMS test of `AudioRecord` before and during a cellular call on a Pixel and a Samsung; whether on-device `SpeechRecognizer` ships French offline.
- Twilio: whether `<Start><Stream>` is blocked on trial; French number monthly price and bundle review time; VoLTE three-way support on T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon, Orange, SFR, Free, Bouygues; caller-ID pass-through on forwarded calls.
- Native: language list of Pixel and S26 call Scam Detection; default state of iOS 26 Call Screening; EU status of iOS call recording.
- The announced-listening framing (feasibility #10, Open) must be settled before the scene is filmed.
