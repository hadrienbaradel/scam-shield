# Scam taxonomies: what exists, what to anchor on, the Prudence family map

Research for the public scam library. All sources accessed 2026-11-04 (see Sources). "(unverified)" = not confirmed on a fetched page.

## 1. Recommendation

No public taxonomy is both senior-readable and complete. The ones with stable identifiers (UK NFIB codes, MITRE ATT&CK) are police/security vocabularies; the senior-readable ones (Scamwatch, cybermalveillance.gouv.fr, Fed ScamClassifier) have no codes, or only URL slugs. So:

- **Own the user-facing layer**: 20 Prudence "families" with slugs we control (column 1 below), organised by *pretext* (what the message pretends to be), not by channel. This is how Scamwatch (ways scammers reach you vs types of scams), the Maldita typology (technique dimension vs thematic dimension) and the Beals/DeLiema/Deevy framework (fraud type vs `Ad:` channel tags) all split it. Channel (email, SMS, call, WhatsApp) stays a separate tag.
- **Anchor the structure on the Federal Reserve ScamClassifier (2024)** for the US side: two categories (Products or Services; Relationship and Trust), nine types (Merchandise, Investment, Property Sale or Rental, Romance Impostor, Government Impostor, Bank Impostor, Business Impostor, Relative/Family/Friend Impostor, Other Trusted Party). It is the only taxonomy built for *the senior's own bank* to classify authorised-push-payment scams, it is impostor-centric (which matches Prudence's "who is this really?" verdict), and it is a strict superset-by-type of the FTC Imposter sub-categories. Every Prudence family maps to exactly one ScamClassifier type.
- **Cross-reference statistics to FBI IC3 crime types** (the only source with 60+ losses per type, yearly) and the **FTC Imposter sub-categories**; cross-reference France to **cybermalveillance.gouv.fr fiche slugs**; cite **NFIB codes** when a stable code is wanted.
- **Keep the Beals/DeLiema/Deevy numbering (1.1 to 1.7) as the academic citation** for "why these families": it is the only hierarchical fraud taxonomy with numeric IDs, built with the FINRA Foundation and tested against FTC Sentinel data. Not maintained since 2015.

Candidate 21st family, senior-specific: **recovery scam** (someone offers to get the lost money back): GASA "Money recover scam", CAFC "Recovery pitch" ($25.9M CA 2025), NFIB1E "Fraud Recovery", Beals 1.2.2.4. IC3 and FTC have no type for it.

Where a family maps to a generic IC3 type (Phishing/Spoofing covers delivery, toll, utility, account), the IC3 numbers cannot be split per family; only FTC Imposter sub-categories and cybermalveillance phishing themes give that granularity.

## 3. Taxonomies found, per source

**FBI IC3 (US)**. 2025 Internet Crime Report (released April 2026): 26 crime types, Appendix B, names only, one type per complaint, "variable and can evolve"; plus 3 descriptors (AI Related, Crimes Against Children, Cryptocurrency). Types: Advanced Fee; BEC; Botnet; Charity; Confidence/Romance; Credit Card/Check Fraud; Data Breach; Employment; Extortion; Government Impersonation; Harassment/Stalking; Identity Theft; Investment; IPR/Copyright and Counterfeit; Lottery/Sweepstakes/Inheritance; Malware; Non-Payment/Non-Delivery; Other; Overpayment; Personal Data Breach; Phishing/Spoofing; Ransomware; Real Estate; SIM Swap; Tech/Customer Support (renamed from "Tech Support" in 2024); Threats of Violence. No standalone Elder Fraud Report since 2023; 60+ data is a section of the annual report. 2025, 60+: 201,266 complaints, $7.748 billion (+59%), average $38,500. 60+ top by loss: Investment $3.52B; Tech/Customer Support $1.04B; Confidence/Romance $584M; BEC $568M; Government Impersonation $413M; Personal Data Breach $324M. 60+ top by count: Phishing/Spoofing 48,064; Tech/Customer Support 21,333; Investment 16,926; Personal Data Breach 11,705; Confidence/Romance 10,188; Non-Payment/Non-Delivery 9,743; Extortion 9,111; Government Impersonation 8,628. All ages 2025: 1,008,597 complaints, $20.9B.

**FTC Consumer Sentinel (US)**. Data Book 2024 (March 2025) is the latest; no 2025 Data Book (404; a 6-week reporting lapse Oct 1 to Nov 13, 2025). 29 named categories, no codes, periodic renames. Top fraud categories by reports 2024: Imposter Scams 845,806; Online Shopping and Negative Reviews 383,441; Business and Job Opportunities 126,217; Investment Related 118,960; Internet Services 118,261; Prizes, Sweepstakes and Lotteries 97,350; Telephone and Mobile Services; Health Care; Travel/Vacations/Timeshare; Mortgage Foreclosure Relief/Debt Management. By losses: Investment Related $5.70B; Imposter Scams $2.95B; Business and Job Opportunities $751M; Online Shopping $432M; Prizes $351M. Imposter sub-categories are published (Appendix B3): Business Imposters 450,104; Government Imposters 265,975; Tech Support 69,221; Romance 59,490; Family & Friend 34,377. 60+ in 2025 (Protecting Older Consumers 2025-2026, Oct 19, 2026): 469,597 reports (33% of aged reports), losses over $3B (from $2.4B in 2024), median about $900, 80+ median $2,000; older adults lose money more often than younger on tech support (+324%), prize/lottery (+112%), romance (+48%), government impersonation (+15%), friend/family impersonation (+15%). 2025 all ages: 3M fraud reports, $15.9B; imposter scams over $3.5B; investment over $7.9B.

**Fed ScamClassifier (US, 2024)**. Step 1 scam? Step 2 authorised vs unauthorised payment; Step 3 category (Products or Services; Relationship and Trust); Step 4 the nine types above. Voluntary, bank-side. Business Impostor examples: tech support, BEC, lottery/prizes, employment offer.

**GASA Global State of Scams 2025** (with Feedzai, 46,000 adults, 42 markets). No formal taxonomy; 12 survey types: Shopping; Investment; Unexpected money; Identity theft; Impersonation; Charity; Fake invoice; Employment; Money recover; Other; Romance/relationship (definition includes family member or friend impostors); Blackmail/extortion. Labels only.

**UK**. NFIB codes (Home Office Counting Rules): the only true stable identifiers. Senior-relevant: NFIB1A 419 advance fee; 1B Lottery; 1D Dating; 1E Fraud Recovery; 1F Inheritance; 1G Rental; 1J Lender Loan; 2A Boiler room; 2B Pyramid/Ponzi; 3A Online Shopping and Auctions; 3B Consumer Phone Fraud; 3C Door to Door / Bogus Tradesmen; 3E Computer Software Service Fraud; 3F Ticket; 4A Charity; 5A Cheque, Plastic Card and Online Bank Accounts; 5D Mandate Fraud; 15 HMRC; 16B Pension fraud on pensioners; 52C Hacking social media and email. UK Finance APP scams: 8 types (purchase, investment, romance, advance fee, invoice and mandate, CEO, impersonation: police/bank staff, impersonation: other), unchanged 2020-2025; 2025: purchase = 71% of cases, investment = 38% of losses. Cifas NFD: facility takeover, identity fraud, misuse of facility, false application, false insurance claims, asset conversion. Stop Scams UK: no taxonomy. ONS Crime Survey: bank and credit account fraud; consumer and retail fraud; advance fee fraud; other fraud (4 buckets, 4.2M incidents YE March 2025).

**Europol IOCTA**. 2024, chapter 4 "Online and payment fraud schemes": phishing (smishing most common, then vishing, spoofing, quishing); account takeover; investment, BEC and romance fraud; digital skimming; ATM attacks. 2025 edition is organised around data, no fraud chapter. No codes.

**Australia Scamwatch**. Two groups on `/types-of-scams` (site 403s to bots; Wayback 2025-06). Ways scammers reach you: `text-or-sms-scams`, `phone-scams`, `email-scams`, `social-media-scams`, `website-scams`, `in-person-scams`. Types: `online-dating-and-romance-scams`, `investment-scams`, `product-and-service-scams`, `threats-and-extortion-scams`, `jobs-and-employment-scams`, `unexpected-money`, `impersonation-scams`. One-line senior-friendly definitions on each. NASC Targeting Scams 2024: investment $945M, romance $156.8M, payment redirection $152.6M, remote access $106M, phishing $84.5M.

**Canada CAFC**. A-Z index with stable bilingual slugs (Bank investigator, Emergency/Grandparent, Extortion incl. sextortion, Investment, Job, Loan/Grant, Merchandise, Phishing, Prize, Recovery pitch, Romance, Service incl. tech support, SIM swap, Spear phishing, Subscription trap...). 2025 top losses: Investments $351M, Spear phishing $67.9M, Relationship $63.3M, Job $50.6M, Fraud investigator $28.3M, Recovery pitch $25.9M. Statistical labels drift ("Bank investigator" became "Fraud investigator").

**Singapore SPF / ScamShield**. Annual brief top 10, 2025: E-commerce; Phishing; Job; Investment ($336M, top loss); Government Officials Impersonation ($243M); Fake Friend Call; Sexual Services; Insurance Services (new); Loan; Internet Love. Labels only.

**Interpol Global Financial Fraud Assessment (March 2024)**. Six offence methods: impersonation fraud; BEC (sub-type assessed separately); investment fraud; romance fraud; advance-payment fraud; identity fraud (via phishing/smishing/vishing). "Pig butchering" renamed "romance baiting" Dec 2024.

**France**. cybermalveillance.gouv.fr: 21 "Particuliers" fiches réflexes (slugs in the table; also `fraude-carte-bancaire`, `usurpation-identite-que-faire`, `que-faire-en-cas-de-fuite-de-donnees-personnelles`, `virus-informatiques`, `rancongiciel-ransomware-particuliers`, `spam-electronique`, `spam-telephonique`, cyberharcèlement); 2025 report top requests: hameçonnage 32.9%, piratage de compte 11.6%, violation de données 6.8%, faux support technique 5.9%, cyberharcèlement 5.3%, faux conseiller bancaire 5% (+159%), fraude au virement 3.4% (+196%), usurpation de numéro +517%; top phishing themes: colis, contravention ANTAI, fausse commande Amazon, impayé autoroute, impôts/Ameli/carte Vitale, SNCF/EDF. No fiche for faux CPF, faux crédit, colis, faux site de vente. service-public.gouv.fr N31138: F34800 hameçonnage, F34000 achat (faux site, faux vendeur), F34019 faux acheteur, F34092 location, F34124 sentiments/sextorsion, F34010 piratage de compte, F34129 rançongiciel, F31324 carte bancaire (Perceval). DGCCRF Task Force guide (2022): 16 fiches (achats en ligne, dropshipping, chèque, épargne/crédit, paiements, FOVI, usurpation, faux sites administratifs, phishing, dons/cagnottes, faux support technique, RGPD, coordonnées bancaires, rançongiciels, MLM, CPF). SignalConso: sector categories (achat-site, internet, demarchage-abusif, banque-assurance-mutuelle, demarches-administratives), not scam types. Signal-Arnaques: 12 report types (site annonce, téléphonique, site frauduleux, financière, PayPal, loterie, don, sentimentale, emploi, identité/phishing, réseaux sociaux, autre). THESEE (décret 2024-867): escroquerie, chantage, extorsion; case types on service-public: faux site, faux vendeur, faux acheteur, fausse location, sentiments, chantage, piratage de messagerie, rançongiciel. OSMP 2024: fraud by instrument; "fraude par manipulation" (faux conseiller) 32%, 382 M€; romance, faux e-commerce, faux investissement, faux crédit excluded from its stats.

**Academic / standards**. MITRE ATT&CK T1566 Phishing (v2.8): .001 Spearphishing Attachment, .002 Link, .003 via Service, .004 Voice; T1598 Phishing for Information: .001 Service, .002 Attachment, .003 Link, .004 Voice. Stable IDs, but technique-level (how), not pretext-level. APWG Phishing Activity Trends Q2 2025: sectors (Financial Institution 18.3%, SaaS/Webmail 18.2%, Payment 12.1%, Social Media 11.3%, Telecom 4.0%, Logistics/Shipping 3.9%, Crypto 2.7%, Other 14.7%) and BEC cash-out methods (Gift card 72%, Payroll diversion 11%, Wire 6%, Interac 6%, Crypto 4%); no scam taxonomy. ENISA ETL 2025: phishing = 60% of intrusion vectors (incl. malspam, vishing, malvertising); threat groups, not scam types. No ISO standard classifies consumer scams. Beals, DeLiema, Deevy, "Framework for a Taxonomy of Fraud" (Stanford Center on Longevity / FINRA Foundation, July 2015), modelled on the UN crime classification: 1.1 Consumer Investment; 1.2 Consumer Products and Services (1.2.1 worthless/non-existent products incl. 1.2.1.2.1 online marketplace; 1.2.2 worthless services incl. 1.2.2.4 fraud loss recovery, 1.2.2.7 fake loans, 1.2.2.9 phishing with 1.2.2.9.1 tech support; 1.2.3 unauthorised billing); 1.3 Employment; 1.4 Prize and Grant (1.4.1 sweepstakes, 1.4.2 lottery, 1.4.3 Nigerian letter, 1.4.4 government grant, 1.4.5 inheritance, 1.4.6 IRS refund); 1.5 Phantom Debt Collection; 1.6 Charity; 1.7 Relationship and Trust (1.7.1 romance, 1.7.2 friends or relatives imposter); plus tags (IG impersonated government official, HM health, CS continuity, OV overpayment; channel Ad:IE, Ad:TX, Ad:DM...). Maldita.es / Faktograf "A Typology of Digital Scams" (Sept 2025): technique axis (1 social engineering: 1.1 phishing, 1.2 smishing, 1.3 vishing, 1.4 baiting, 1.5 QRshing, 1.6 catfishing; 2 password; 3 connection incl. 3.1 spoofing; 4 malware) x thematic axis (promotions/offers; contests/prizes; inheritance/donations/lottery; purchase or sale; property rentals; loans; investments; gambling; threats and extortion; job offers; romantic relationship; work environment; notifications from a service or institution; other), multi-label. Agarwal, Suarez-Tangil, Vasek (arXiv 2508.05276, 2025), 213,659 UK 7726 scam texts: Wrong Number 16.4%, Banking 9.1%, Delivery/Parcel 6.8%, Job 5.1%, Debt 4.9%, Hi Mum/Dad 3.2%, Telecom 2.7%, Appointment 2.1%, Government 1.9%, Finance 1.8%, Utility 1.2%, Insurance 0.6%. Stajano & Wilson (CACM 2011): seven principles (distraction, social compliance, herd, dishonesty, deception, need and greed, time), a victim-psychology lens, not a scam list. OECD "Protecting Consumers from Financial Scams and Frauds" (June 2026): ten-dimensional typology for regulators (page 403 to fetch; dimensions unverified).

## 4. Stable identifiers, summary

Stable codes: NFIB (NFIBnX), MITRE ATT&CK (T1566.00x), Beals 2015 numbering (frozen). Stable-ish URL slugs: Scamwatch, cybermalveillance.gouv.fr, CAFC, service-public F-numbers. Names only, renamed over time: IC3, FTC, GASA, UK Finance, SPF, Europol, Interpol, Fed ScamClassifier.
