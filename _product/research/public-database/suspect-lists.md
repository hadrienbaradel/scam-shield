# Public scam-suspect database: what exists, what we may reuse, what it costs legally

Research for Prudence, 2026-11-04. Every claim carries a source in the Sources section (all accessed 2026-11-04). "(unverified)" = not confirmed at the source. Not legal advice.

## Bottom line

- Nothing free, redistributable and machine-readable exists that pairs a scam phone number or URL with the message that used it, as a live feed. Two static research datasets come close (section 5).
- Reusable today without a contract: FTC Do Not Call complaints and FCC Unwanted Calls complaints (US public domain, numbers only, unverified), Phishing.Database (MIT, domains/links only), PhishTank hourly dump (FAQ allows commercial use, but no new accounts since 2020), teleph0ne-spam-list (ODbL, 394 numbers), Smishing-Dataset-IMC25 (CC BY 4.0, static).
- Everything else is non-commercial, no-redistribution or paid: OpenPhish, URLhaus (non-profit only, no AI training), Spamhaus, SURBL, Netcraft, APWG, Cloudflare Radar (CC BY-NC), urlscan, CrowdSec, AbuseIPDB, ScamAdviser, tellows, Should I Answer, 800notes, Truecaller, Hiya, Nomorobo (except its CC BY 4.0 IRS feed).
- Publishing "this number is a scam" from France is lawful in principle (legitimate interest + freedom of expression, Signal-Arnaques won on appeal in 2023) but the operator is liable the moment it ignores a precise notice (CA Reims 2018, CA Montpellier 2012, T. com. Paris 2019 and 2021). In the US, Section 230 covers user submissions, never our own labels.
- Best GitHub precedents: MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect (process), Phishing-Database (contribution repo split from artefact, DNS-TXT ownership proof), haumacher/phoneblock (phone numbers as decaying votes, k-anonymous lookup).

## 4. Legal exposure (France and US)

A. GDPR
- A phone number, email or IBAN tied to a person is personal data. Signal-Arnaques (controller EXODUS EDITIONS SAS) claims "intérêt public légitime" and freedom of expression, table citing art. 6.1.f, for publishing and moderating reports; retention "tant qu'ils demeurent pertinents pour l'information du public"; erasure limited to its §7 bis conditions; deleting your account anonymises but keeps reports. Truecaller: "We consider that we have a legitimate interest to collect spam reports made voluntarily by our users". tellows's policy cites 6(1)(f) only for storing IPs; no stated basis for publishing numbers.
- Art. 17(1)(c) erasure applies when the person objects (art. 21) and "there are no overriding legitimate grounds"; art. 17(3)(a) exempts processing "for exercising the right of freedom of expression and information". That is the hinge every site relies on.
- CNIL: directory data comes from operators under CPCE art. L.34; mobile numbers are out of directories by default and anyone can opt out of reverse lookup. CNIL's blacklist doctrine (2009): lists "ne peuvent être secrètes", limited to incidents "d'une gravité certaine et prédéterminée", access "limités à un secteur et aux seuls professionnels du secteur", person informed, right of opposition, "Pas de mise au pilori électronique". No CNIL position on community scam-report sites found; no CNIL action against Signal-Arnaques or tellows found (unverified). Swedish IMY closed its Truecaller GDPR investigation on 2025-09-05 with no breach found. No German court ruling on tellows found.
- Reading: a public list of numbers used for scams sits uneasily with "no electronic pillory" unless entries are about businesses/numbers, not named persons, with a visible removal path. Spoofed caller IDs mean a listed number can be a victim's.

B. Defamation and host liability
- France, diffamation (loi 1881): 3-month limitation, intent presumed, "bonne foi" to prove. Courts requalify attacks on a company's products as dénigrement (art. 1240 C. civ.), which escapes the 1881 procedure: T. com. Paris 2019-12-16 (Signal-Arnaques: article + comments "bonne grosse arnaque", removal under 500 EUR/day, 5,000 EUR damages, because of unanswered mises en demeure under LCEN art. 6-I-2); CA Reims 2018-07-10 (Soluance/118816 v. Hérétic: dénigrement not diffamation, host liable only for illicit content it knew of from a precise notice, blanket removal refused, "dénoncer publiquement la cherté d'un service est la manifestation de sa liberté d'expression"); T. com. Paris référé 2021-12-22 (MAC Assistance: six pages removed because reviews lacked the dates required by C. conso. L.111-7-2 / D.111-17, and sites "détruire très rapidement la réputation d'une entreprise sans apporter la moindre preuve"); CA Paris 2023-09-07 (dénigrement rejected: "arnaque" read in its broad sense, not escroquerie; virulent reviews "n'apparaissent pas dépasser la libre critique"; the company had not used the reply tools). LesArnaques.com: CA Montpellier 2012-11-14, 11,000 EUR, because the publication director "n'a pas supprimé promptement les messages incriminés en dépit des réclamations".
- Hébergeur vs éditeur: Cass. 2011 requires no "rôle actif"; moderation and formatting alone do not create one; Cass. 2024-03-27 n° 22-21.586: a host may be ordered to remove but not to monitor generally. Signal-Arnaques keeps host status by publishing "sans qu'une quelconque modération ne soit réalisée avant publication". Our own verdict label ("scam", a score) is our content, not hosted content.
- US: Section 230 immunizes the operator for user posts, even when it screens, edits or encourages them, but "does not immunize the actual creator of content"; the "material contribution" test (Roommates, Jones v. Dirty World, 6th Cir. 2014). 800notes: Vision Media v. Forte (S.D. Fla. 2010) dismissed for lack of personal jurisdiction after Forte pleaded §230; Houlihan Smith v. Forte (2010) dismissed 2010-08-06, settled 2011, relabelled trademark claims did not defeat immunity. No suit found against Hiya, TNS, First Orion or Truecaller over "Scam Likely" labels (TCPAWorld 2022 noted the absence; nothing newer found).

C. EU Digital Services Act (Reg. 2022/2065)
- A site taking public submissions is at least a hosting service and, since it disseminates to the public, an online platform. Art. 19 exempts micro/small enterprises only from Section 3 (art. 20-28, except 24(3)); art. 15(2) exempts them from transparency reports. Art. 11-14 and 16-18 still apply: points of contact, terms, notice-and-action with (a) reasons, (b) exact URL, (c) notifier name/email, (d) good-faith statement; decision notified with redress (16(5)), handled "in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary and objective manner" (16(6)); statement of reasons (art. 17). France: loi SREN n° 2024-449 (2024-05-21) makes Arcom the Digital Services Coordinator (new LCEN art. 7), with DGCCRF and CNIL.

D. Procedures in practice (details in section 2): tellows reasoned per-comment requests, 48 h author reply, no ownership check; Signal-Arnaques identity + URL + legal basis + prior contact, public moderation report; Truecaller irreversible self-unlisting, email for wrong labels; 800notes refuses truth-based removal; Should I Answer hides on flag. Nobody verifies who owns a number.

E. Other cases: Amadeus Mobile (118001) sued HERETIC SAS in June 2017 for ~50,000 EUR; outcome not found (unverified). No case found against ScamAdviser or tellows.

## 5. Does the exact thing exist?

No. No player offers a free, redistributable, machine-readable feed of scam numbers or sites with the message that used them.
- Closest, static: reportsmishing/Smishing-Dataset-IMC25 (UCL, CC BY 4.0, Sept 2025: labelled smishing messages mined from public forums, with sender IDs and URLs; one CSV, no submission channel). Smishtank (CSU San Marcos, 1,090 community-submitted SMS with sender, body, brand, VirusTotal data; smishtank.com/dataset returns 404 today, licence unverified).
- Closest, live but without text: FTC DNC and FCC Unwanted Calls (public domain, numbers + subject category); Nomorobo's IRS feed (CC BY 4.0, gated by a form).
- Closest with evidence but not open: Signal-Arnaques (SMS text per report, no API, no licence), Nomorobo lookup pages (recording + transcript, ToS forbids scraping), urlscan (screenshots, no redistribution), APWG eCX (emails, paid).
- Closest community-run and open: PhoneBlock (free API, votes with decay, data licence unstated), teleph0ne-spam-list (ODbL, tiny), Phishing.Database (MIT, no evidence).
Implication: a public repo pairing indicator + redacted message + verdict rationale under ODbL or CC BY-SA, with DSA-style notice forms and a decay score, would be the first of its kind; the licence and removal design, not the data, is the novelty.
