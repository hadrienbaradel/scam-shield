Keywords: naming, name, trademark, app store, aso, domains, scamshield

Can we keep "Scam Shield", and if not, what should the product be called? Checked 2026-10-27 on the US and French App Store (iTunes Search API), Google Play search, US trademarks (Trademarkia, a USPTO mirror; top ~30 results per query, exact phrases only, so medium confidence) and domain registries (RDAP: 404 = likely unregistered). EU and French trademarks (EUIPO, INPI): not checked (unverified).

## Findings
- **"Scam Shield" cannot go public.** T-Mobile holds the registered mark SCAMSHIELD (Reg. 6437351, classes 9 and 38, live); Singapore's government ships a "ScamShield" app; "ScamShield Family" is another team in this hackathon.
- **Vlad's candidates are all taken in our space:** Second Opinion (medical apps on both stores, 100+ US marks incl. a live class 42), Lantern (a top VPN app with live class 9/42 marks; Lantern PBC is an end-of-life planning service, a death association for a 70+ audience), Kinsafe ("KinSafe Check-In", July 2026, for checking on elderly relatives; live class 9 mark), Trustline (live class 42 mark and AI-service filings; California's TrustLine is a childcare background-check registry), Inbox Guardian (a French Gmail anti-phishing extension of that name exists; "inbox" also leaves out texts and voicemail).
- **Hadrien's candidates:** Grandma/Grandpa Defend(er) is legally the cleanest (no app, no mark found; grandmadefender.com free, verified) but labels the user by age and role, "granny scam" is the common name of the grandparent scam, "granny" searches on both stores return a horror game, Meta ads may not assert or imply a viewer's age, and "Grandma Buddy" is another team here. Family Defender (live class 36 mark; a "Family Defender Technologies LLC" ships a family-safety app) and Scam Defender (an existing AI scam detector) are taken; Shield/Guard/Defender names are saturated.
- **"Scam" in a name is allowed** on both stores and in ads; what is banned is overclaiming. Apple 2.3.7 asks for a unique name and no trademarked or popular terms in metadata, 2.3.1(a) removes apps advertising services they do not offer, 2.5.12 asks spam-identification apps to explain their criteria; Google Play forbids false or misleading claims and impersonation; Google Ads forbids improbable results ("blocks 100% of scams"). Big vendors keep a short brand and put "scam" in a feature name (Bitdefender Scamio, McAfee Scam Detector, Trend Micro ScamCheck).
- **Senior products avoid age words:** Carefull (care + careful), EverSafe (developer "Elderscan LLC", brand without "elder"); the Reframing Aging guide prefers "older adults"; MIT AgeLab: older consumers "flee any product giving off a whiff of 'oldness'".

## Recommendation
1. **Prudence**: the same word and meaning in English and French, dignified, also reads as a helper's name ("Ask Prudence"). No security app of that name on the US App Store (top results are school apps, verified). prudence.com and prudence.app are taken; **heyprudence.com and heyprudence.app are free** (verified). Drawback: in French it reads a little like a road warning sign. Store names: "Prudence: Is It a Scam?" / subtitle "Check with family first"; French "Prudence : arnaque ou pas ?" / "Vérifiez avec un proche"; Google Play "Prudence: Scam Check & Family" (29 characters).
2. **Ask Twice** (English only): the name is the product loop (pause, then ask your person); no app or mark found; asktwice.com is taken, **getasktwice.app is free** (verified). Store name "Ask Twice: Scam Checker" / "A second look from family". Also works as Prudence's tagline and the label on the trusted-person button.

Before a real launch: EUIPO/INPI search in classes 9 and 42 and a short attorney clearance. Keep every claim modest ("explains", "helps you check").
