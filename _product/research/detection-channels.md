# Detection channels: how Scam Shield can see each message, per platform
Answers, dated 2026-10-28, how Scam Shield can see and judge a scam across email, SMS and messaging, voicemail, live calls, landlines, web pages and a "send me something" path: what each platform lets a third party intercept, where the senior sees the verdict, what each option needs, which external gate stands in front of it, and which options fit before the Nov 4 deadline. Purely technical; typologies, statistics and consumer law are out of scope. Figures come from the ten detail files in `research/detection-channels/` (the iPhone figures from the raw iOS note, condensed in [ios-hooks](detection-channels/ios-hooks.md)). The Days column of the matrix stays as a record of the researchers' estimates, but build time is not what decides the video: with coding agents the build is fast, and an option is in or out by its external gate (an approval, a verification, a review, a number provisioning, an account, hardware delivery, legal clearance), re-cut in the Summary and in Gates and lead times below.

## Platform answer
- iPhone lets a third party intercept almost nothing: a Message Filter sorts SMS/MMS/RCS from non-contacts into folders with no card, CallKit exposes no call audio and no outgoing-call hook, the default browser needs a managed entitlement, Web Share Target does not exist in WebKit, and a native app needs a Mac, a 7-day free profile or the paid program. What the senior (or an automation) can forward is wide: a screenshot of any app through a Shortcut on Back Tap or the Action Button, the sender and text of every incoming SMS and iMessage through the Message automation, a Safari page, a photo or a voicemail .m4a through the Share Sheet, a dictated question through Siri. Recommended iPhone path: the Shortcuts pair (screenshot shortcut plus Message automation, about 2 d with the device-token image endpoint) ending on the web card, plus the Gmail add-on card for mail; say "iPhone sorts, Scam Shield explains".
- Android lets a sideloaded app intercept: incoming SMS (`RECEIVE_SMS`, a restricted setting on file installs since 15), RCS, WhatsApp, Messenger and Telegram through the notification listener (one toggle; OTP notifications redacted on 15+), any screen through the assistant role, every dialed number through `CallRedirectionService`, every tapped link through the browser role, and unknown incoming calls through call screening (no audio, ever). Recommended Android path: full-screen intent plus share target now, the notification listener next, then the link gate; never the default SMS role, never an AccessibilityService.
- Desktop is where email is read in a client and where scare pages happen: one unpacked MV3 extension puts a bar in Gmail and can overlay any page in Chrome or Edge; the Outlook task pane and MailKit need accounts, lead time or both; the Gmail add-on card is the only tappable surface shared by web, Android and iOS. Mobile mail clients mark but never interrupt; interruption comes from the companion on Android and from Shortcuts notifications on iPhone.
- The web app can show the card, read it aloud, carry "Ask my trusted person", take a camera photo of a letter, listen on a second device (`getUserMedia`, Web Speech API or the Muse Voice Transcribe relay) and receive web push on Android (on iOS after a Home Screen install and a tap); it cannot receive shares on iPhone, so the shortcuts are the bridge.
- Public URL: forward-to-check@ on the polled Gmail mailbox, the IMAP connector, Graph delta polling, Telegram long polling and the share upload need none; the add-on, the Outlook pane, every Twilio, WhatsApp and Messenger webhook, the extension's calls, Alexa and the Siri POST need the tunnel (feasibility #25).
- Mobile vs desktop for the video: the phone scenes (Android full-screen verdict, iPhone shortcut, voicemail share) prove interception and the "ask" path; the desktop scenes (Gmail labels, extension bar) prove the mail client. Same engine, same card, same guardian loop on every surface.
