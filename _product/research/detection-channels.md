# Detection channels: how Scam Shield can see each message, per platform
Answers, dated 2026-10-28, how Scam Shield can see and judge a scam across email, SMS and messaging, voicemail, live calls, landlines, web pages and a "send me something" path: what each platform lets a third party intercept, where the senior sees the verdict, what each option needs, how many days it takes, and which options fit before the Nov 4 deadline. Purely technical; typologies, statistics and consumer law are out of scope. Figures come from the ten detail files in `research/detection-channels/` (the iPhone figures from the raw iOS note, condensed in [ios-hooks](detection-channels/ios-hooks.md)); day figures are estimates for this two-person team on the existing code. Assumption: the video is recorded on the last two or three days, so about four engineering days remain for new building between now and the recording.

## Summary
Feasible by Nov 4 (Yes in both columns of the matrix; option, days, the scene it adds):
- Email: forward-to-check@ on a polled Gmail mailbox, 0.5 d (1-1.5 d with a branded Resend address): any mailbox, any phone forwards a mail and the verdict mail comes back; Gmail labels, built (0.5 d to verify the chip on a phone); Gmail add-on card, 1.5-2 d: on a phone, open the mail, tap the icon, "This is a scam", "Ask my daughter"; Chrome InboxSDK bar, 1-2 d: desktop red bar under the subject with the buttons inline.

- iPhone: screenshot shortcut on Back Tap, Action Button or Lock Screen, 0.5-1 d: "something feels off? double-tap the back" from WhatsApp, Mail or Messages, verdict spoken; Message automation, 0.5-1 d: a text arrives, the phone speaks "Be careful" and the notification opens the card; Share Sheet shortcut, 0.25 d: a Safari page or a photo shared to Scam Shield; Siri dictation shortcut, 0.5 d: "Siri, is this a scam?".
- Voice and phone: voicemail share or upload with Muse Voice Transcribe, 1 d (+0.5 Android, +0.5 iOS): "Grandma shares the bank voicemail", two Meta models on screen; live-call fallback on the browser's Web Speech API, 0.5-1 d: speakerphone scene without the Meta transcription credit; landline voicemail-to-e-mail read by the Gmail sync, 1-2 d: "Message left on your home phone" card; operator checklist, 0.5 d: one caption; Alexa custom skill, 1 d: 10 s kitchen scene with no phone.
- Ask and protect: Telegram bot, 0.5-1 d: off-camera harness (plus 0.5 d family group); fridge QR to a no-sign-in check page, 0.5 d plus the public URL: Françoise scans the magnet and photographs a letter; relative on the senior's behalf, 0.5 d: the daughter forwards, the grandmother's screen shows the verdict; "Protect this phone" checklist, 0.5 d: the daughter flips five switches; NextDNS denylist push, 1 d plus 0.5 d guide: "blocked on every device at home"; Firefox port, 0.5-1 d, no new scene.

Feasible later (blocker in parentheses): Gmail over IMAP (Restricted scope review; never build, worse than the API); Apple Mail on Mac via MailKit (Developer Program, signing lead time); public Gmail add-on, Outlook add-in, Chrome Web Store and Edge listings (verification and review, days to weeks); Safari extension on Mac, iPhone and iPad (program, TestFlight review); default SMS app (7-10 d; removes Google Messages); Play internal test track (policy: drop `RECEIVE_SMS` first); Live Caller ID Lookup (Apple endpoint validation, PIR stack); SMS replies via Twilio in the US (toll-free verification, 3 business days) and France (number lead time, weeks); RCS Business Messaging (launch approval); WhatsApp Calling API (2,000-recipient tier); voicemail answering agent on ConversationRelay (3-5 d; French number, legal disclosure); hotline on ConversationRelay (2-3 d, non-Meta voice stack); US simultaneous-ring screener and unconditional forward (3-4 d each, a US VoIP line); iOS safe browser (managed entitlement); payment moment (aggregator contracts); Meta AI Connectors, Gemini AppFunctions, glasses voice launch (previews and lead times).

Not possible (the platform blocks it): listening to a call from the senior's own phone (Android mutes ordinary apps during a call, iOS suspends them); iOS outgoing-call gate (CallKit has no hook); Web Share Target on iPhone (WebKit never implemented it); a client hook in Apple Mail on iPhone (no extension API); ISP webmail add-ons (no API at Orange, Free, SFR, La Poste, Yahoo); extensions in Chrome or Edge on Android (none); an iMessage bot (Apple-approved MSP enterprise program only); Signal (no official API); a Scam Shield number inside a family WhatsApp group (groups are business-created, Official Business Account only); reading voicemail on Android without becoming the default dialer (`READ_VOICEMAIL` is dialer-only); Bixby capsules and Nest Hub voice apps (closed).

## Platform answer
- iPhone lets a third party intercept almost nothing: a Message Filter sorts SMS/MMS/RCS from non-contacts into folders with no card, CallKit exposes no call audio and no outgoing-call hook, the default browser needs a managed entitlement, Web Share Target does not exist in WebKit, and a native app needs a Mac, a 7-day free profile or the paid program. What the senior (or an automation) can forward is wide: a screenshot of any app through a Shortcut on Back Tap or the Action Button, the sender and text of every incoming SMS and iMessage through the Message automation, a Safari page, a photo or a voicemail .m4a through the Share Sheet, a dictated question through Siri. Recommended iPhone path: the Shortcuts pair (screenshot shortcut plus Message automation, about 2 d with the device-token image endpoint) ending on the web card, plus the Gmail add-on card for mail; say "iPhone sorts, Scam Shield explains".
- Android lets a sideloaded app intercept: incoming SMS (`RECEIVE_SMS`, a restricted setting on file installs since 15), RCS, WhatsApp, Messenger and Telegram through the notification listener (one toggle; OTP notifications redacted on 15+), any screen through the assistant role, every dialed number through `CallRedirectionService`, every tapped link through the browser role, and unknown incoming calls through call screening (no audio, ever). Recommended Android path: full-screen intent plus share target now, the notification listener next, then the link gate; never the default SMS role, never an AccessibilityService.
- Desktop is where email is read in a client and where scare pages happen: one unpacked MV3 extension puts a bar in Gmail and can overlay any page in Chrome or Edge; the Outlook task pane and MailKit need accounts, lead time or both; the Gmail add-on card is the only tappable surface shared by web, Android and iOS. Mobile mail clients mark but never interrupt; interruption comes from the companion on Android and from Shortcuts notifications on iPhone.
- The web app can show the card, read it aloud, carry "Ask my trusted person", take a camera photo of a letter, listen on a second device (`getUserMedia`, Web Speech API or the Muse Voice Transcribe relay) and receive web push on Android (on iOS after a Home Screen install and a tap); it cannot receive shares on iPhone, so the shortcuts are the bridge.
- Public URL: forward-to-check@ on the polled Gmail mailbox, the IMAP connector, Graph delta polling, Telegram long polling and the share upload need none; the add-on, the Outlook pane, every Twilio, WhatsApp and Messenger webhook, the extension's calls, Alexa and the Siri POST need the tunnel (feasibility #25).
- Mobile vs desktop for the video: the phone scenes (Android full-screen verdict, iPhone shortcut, voicemail share) prove interception and the "ask" path; the desktop scenes (Gmail labels, extension bar) prove the mail client. Same engine, same card, same guardian loop on every surface.

## Corrections to earlier research
One bullet per feasibility row, merged from the detail files; the same texts are applied in `feasibility.md`.
- #2: Outside Gmail, IDLE is advertised only by Yahoo/AOL and Outlook.com; the French ISPs and iCloud show none pre-login, so promise "about two minutes" there and under one minute only with Graph webhooks (public HTTPS).
- #3: IMAP is no way around verification: `https://mail.google.com/` is Restricted too and demands "full utilization"; app passwords need 2-Step Verification and die on a password change. An unverified production app runs behind the warning screen, capped at 100 new users.
- #4: Add-on cards render on web and phone apps (Sensitive `message.metadata` suffices); the InboxSDK App ID is free; one MV3 extension serves Gmail and any page and `activeTab` installs without warning; Web Store review takes days to weeks; the Outlook add-in twin needs a public HTTPS origin and never activates on a `.eml`.
- #6: A sideloaded `RECEIVE_SMS` APK on Android 15/16 needs one "Allow restricted settings" tap (adb bypasses it); Advanced Protection and Auto Blocker block sideloaded installs and updates; Android 16 blocks a first sideload during a non-contact call. Install the APK first.
- #7: The notification listener is the real third-party RCS route (one toggle, OTP notifications redacted on 15+). iOS 26 Screen Unknown Senders hides unknown texts natively; a third-party Message Filter never applies to senders replied to three or more times.
- #8: Android call screening also sees outgoing non-contact calls and STIR/SHAKEN; `CallRedirectionService` intercepts dialed numbers (5 s, no audio); voicemail audio is reachable by share, carrier email or Twilio; Pixel 9+ call Scam Detection runs in 12 countries, iOS 26 adds Call Screening (EN, FR); landlines add no-answer forwarding and US simultaneous ring.

Muse Voice Transcribe covers French, $0.18/h, 60-minute sessions; Twilio official: inbound $0.0085, Media Streams $0.0044, ConversationRelay $0.07/min with non-Meta STT and TTS; the glasses microphone is beamformed to the wearer.
- #10: EU AI Act art. 50(1), in force 2 Aug 2026, obliges an answering agent to disclose the AI; FCC 24-17 treats AI voices as artificial (outbound). A voicemail played by its recipient and an answering number that records only the caller after an announcement are the lightest setups; counsel still needed. Stays Open.
- #11: Meta's glasses Acceptable Use Policy (Sep 22, 2026) bans identifying people by voice or face without prior approval, so no "is this really my grandson" check may run on glasses audio.
- #12: Live advice can use `minimal` (2.8 s): a rule cue 1-2 s after the ask from partial transcripts, the Muse line 4-7 s after the sentence; STT partial and endpointing delays are unmeasured estimates.
- #13: No mail client lets a third party interrupt; interruption stays with the companion and web push. - #14: On iPhone the Gmail add-on card gives a tappable in-place verdict with no native app, and a Siri dictation shortcut gives hands-free voice entry (0.5 d); "no native iOS before Nov 4" stands.
